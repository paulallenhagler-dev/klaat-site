KLAAT Resonance Research — Session 52 TACTILE NEGATIVE SPACE: Making
Ankora Feel Stronger by Giving the Pulse Somewhere to Land


Session 51 established a compositional principle that matters deeply to
KLAAT: repetition is not empty time. A stable field teaches the listener
what to expect, and a later omission or deviation can become
perceptually large without adding physical energy.


The strongest KLAAT-relevant finding in the new hourly research extends
that principle from temporal expectation into the body.


The question is deceptively simple:


If Amastacia is already producing a sustained bed of felt vibration,
does adding more Ankora vibration always make the Pulse feel stronger?


The evidence says we should not assume so.


In tactile perception, a background vibration can make another vibration
harder to detect. This is vibrotactile masking. The effect depends on
timing, level, frequency, body site, contact conditions, and which
tactile pathways are being stimulated. A sufficiently strong or
prolonged masker can raise the threshold for a subsequent tactile
signal, and forward masking generally weakens as more time separates the
masker from the test signal.


For KLAAT, that suggests a new possibility:


ANKORA MAY SOMETIMES BECOME MORE BODILY LEGIBLE WHEN AMASTACIA
MOMENTARILY GIVES IT SPACE.


This is not a claim that Amastacia and Ankora are currently masking one
another in live performance. We have not measured that. It is a
mechanism strong enough to test.


1.  MORE VIBRATION IS NOT NECESSARILY MORE PULSE


KLAAT has repeatedly encountered the temptation to treat bodily
intensity as a single quantity: more low-frequency energy, more floor
movement, more contact vibration, therefore more somatic impact.


That model is too simple.


Imagine a continuous mechanical background produced by Amastacia. Ankora
then adds a transient whose energy overlaps the same tactile channel.
The nervous system does not necessarily experience the transient as
“background plus new signal” with perfect separation. The background can
reduce the perceptual contrast of the transient.


The useful analogy is not loudness. It is visibility.


A bright flash is conspicuous in darkness and less conspicuous against
another bright source. The flash itself has not changed. The background
has changed the contrast.


Tactile perception has analogous masking and increment-detection
effects.


So KLAAT gains a new variable:


TACTILE SIGNAL-TO-BACKGROUND RELATIONSHIP.


This is not simply the ratio of two accelerometer numbers. It is a
perceptual relationship whose physical contributors include magnitude,
frequency overlap, temporal overlap, contact pathway, and sensory
adaptation.


2.  FORWARD MASKING GIVES TIME A PHYSICAL ROLE


Classic vibrotactile work has shown that a preceding masker can impair
detection of a later tactile target and that the effect depends strongly
on the interval between masker offset and target onset. Masker amplitude
and duration also matter.


That gives KLAAT a remarkable compositional possibility.


A short reduction in Amastacia-derived mechanical background immediately
before an Ankora strike might make the strike feel more distinct even if
Ankora itself is not played harder.


The important structure would be:


DRONE → MICRO-OPENING → PULSE.


The micro-opening does not have to be silence. It could be a brief
reduction in the component of Amastacia that is coupling most strongly
into a particular surface or body pathway.


This is what we will call TACTILE NEGATIVE SPACE.


The term is a KLAAT compositional construct, not a standard scientific
metric.


Tactile negative space means:


A deliberately created reduction in competing mechanical/tactile
background that increases the perceptual contrast available to a
subsequent event.


The artistic consequence is substantial.


KLAAT may be able to increase bodily definition by subtracting energy
before a Pulse rather than adding energy to the Pulse.


3.  FREQUENCY OVERLAP MATTERS


Tactile masking is not completely frequency-blind. Research on
vibrotactile masking shows frequency-selective behavior under some
conditions: masking tends to be stronger when masker and target engage
overlapping tactile processing channels, although the exact pattern
depends on frequency range, body site, stimulus geometry, and intensity.


That matters because Amastacia and Ankora do not produce single
frequencies.


Amastacia’s nominal C# identity does not mean the body receives only one
sinusoid. The instrument produces a harmonic and time-varying spectrum,
and the room/structure can selectively reinforce parts of it.


Ankora’s attack is even broader. Its transient contains a changing
spectrum whose mechanical transmission depends on drum technique, source
coupling, floor construction, listener contact, and position.


Therefore the useful KLAAT question is not:


“Does C# mask the drum?”


It is:


“AT THE LISTENER’S ACTUAL CONTACT PATHWAY, WHICH AMASTACIA-DERIVED
VIBRATION BANDS OVERLAP THE MECHANICALLY SALIENT PARTS OF ANKORA’S
ATTACK?”


That is a measurement question, not a symbolic-frequency question.


If overlap is substantial, a narrow reduction in Amastacia’s
mechanically transmitted energy around that region might improve Pulse
definition without requiring a broadband volume dip.


If overlap is weak, the tactic may do little.


4.  THE BODY DOES NOT HAVE ONE TACTILE CHANNEL


The hourly research also places a hard boundary around this idea.


Results from fingertip psychophysics cannot simply be transplanted onto
a standing or seated KLAAT listener.


Whole-body and body-site vibration sensitivity varies with frequency,
posture, direction, contact force, and body location. The feet,
pelvis/back, and torso are not interchangeable sensors. Standing can
change foot sensitivity relative to sitting. Torso sites differ from one
another. Whole-body vertical vibration has its own frequency-dependent
perceptual behavior.


Therefore “Amastacia masks Ankora” is too broad to be a useful
hypothesis.


The correct KLAAT hypothesis is pathway-specific:


Amastacia may mask some mechanically transmitted features of Ankora
through a particular body-contact pathway under particular conditions.


This is why Sessions 49 and 50 mattered.


Audio–tactile coherence told us that timing between channels may matter.


Body–surface coupling told us that the interface changes what reaches
the listener.


Session 52 now adds:


BACKGROUND–TARGET CONTRAST MAY DETERMINE HOW CLEARLY THAT COUPLED PULSE
IS FELT.


These are not three unrelated technical lessons. They are becoming one
KLAAT model.


5.  AMASTACIA AS FIELD, ANKORA AS FIGURE


This suggests a particularly useful way to conceptualize the two core
instruments.


AMASTACIA creates FIELD.


ANKORA creates FIGURE.


That is not absolute. Amastacia can articulate and Ankora can create
sustained resonance. But as a compositional model it is powerful.


A figure becomes legible partly because it differs from its field.


If the field fills every available sensory dimension at maximum density,
the figure may have less perceptual room.


KLAAT therefore does not necessarily want Amastacia to maximize bodily
vibration continuously.


A more sophisticated Drone may establish a heavy, convincing field while
retaining the ability to withdraw selectively.


That withdrawal can become part of the Pulse.


This changes the relationship between the instruments:


Ankora does not merely enter ON TOP OF Amastacia.


Amastacia can actively SHAPE THE SPACE INTO WHICH ANKORA ENTERS.


That is a more integrated composition system.


6.  MICRO-DIPS ARE NOT YET A PERFORMANCE RULE


The hourly research proposed brief pre-Pulse reductions on the order of
tens to low hundreds of milliseconds as an experimental range.


That is reasonable for testing, but it must not become doctrine.


Laboratory forward-masking timing depends on the exact stimulus and
tactile system being studied. We do not yet know the recovery time of
KLAAT’s floor-, seat-, or torso-mediated pathways.


A 50 ms dip may be effective.


A 150 ms dip may be effective.


Either could be too short, too long, or musically obvious.


The effect could also arise acoustically rather than tactilely if the
listener simply hears Amastacia duck before Ankora.


That confound is important.


If Paul intentionally lowers Amastacia before every strike, the ear
receives a dynamic contrast too. A listener might report a stronger
Pulse because of auditory contrast, tactile contrast, expectation, or
all three.


KLAAT should not pretend these are separated unless the experiment
actually separates them.


7.  THE FOUR-PHASE ARCHITECTURE


GROUND ANCHOR — BUILD THE FIELD


Ground Anchor establishes Amastacia as a continuous sensory reference.
For tactile-negative-space research, the important variable is not
maximum vibration but a sufficiently stable background from which later
contrast can emerge.


The Drone teaches the body what “continuous” feels like.


THRESHOLD GATE — CREATE THE FIRST OPENING


Threshold Gate can be produced not only by Ankora entering but by
Amastacia making room for Ankora.


A brief withdrawal immediately before the first Pulse may create a
double transition:


FIELD WEAKENS → PULSE ARRIVES.


The Gate is therefore carved before it is struck.


RISING PULSE — SCULPT CONTRAST, NOT JUST DENSITY


This is where the concept becomes most KLAAT-specific.


Rising Pulse does not have to climb by hitting harder and harder. It can
increase definition by manipulating the relationship between continuous
and transient energy.


Possible strategies include:


continuous Amastacia + Ankora;


small pre-Pulse Amastacia reductions;


selective spectral thinning around some strikes;


alternating high-overlap and low-overlap passages;


periods where Ankora is physically strong but perceptually embedded;


periods where the same Ankora level emerges from newly created negative
space.


This gives Rising Pulse another axis besides tempo, density, and
loudness:


CONTRAST.


RETURN PATH — LET THE FIELD RECLAIM THE FIGURE


Return Path can reverse the process.


Instead of simply slowing Ankora, Amastacia can gradually stop yielding
space around it. Pulse definition may soften before Ankora itself
disappears.


Then Ankora is removed completely, leaving only the field.


That creates a potentially elegant perceptual trajectory:


FIELD → FIGURE EMERGES → FIGURE SHARPENS → FIGURE REABSORBS → FIELD.


That architecture is recognizably KLAAT.


8.  ACTIVATION WITHOUT ESCALATION


This session reinforces one of the most important developing principles
in the entire research program:


KLAAT SHOULD NOT REQUIRE CONTINUOUS ENERGY ESCALATION TO CREATE
CONTINUOUS PERCEPTUAL DEVELOPMENT.


If tactile negative space works, Rising Pulse can become more bodily
articulated while:


Ankora strike level remains stable,


overall acoustic exposure remains similar or decreases,


and total mechanical energy may even fall briefly before important
strikes.


That is a stronger compositional system than “make it louder.”


It also aligns with Session 51’s missing-beat logic.


Both mechanisms exploit subtraction.


Session 51: remove an expected EVENT.


Session 52: remove part of the BACKGROUND before an event.


One manipulates temporal prediction.


The other manipulates sensory contrast.


They may interact, but they are not the same mechanism.


9.  EVIDENCE BOUNDARY


ESTABLISHED


Vibrotactile masking exists. A tactile masker can raise detection
thresholds for another tactile signal.


ESTABLISHED


Forward masking depends on timing. Masking generally decreases as the
interval between masker offset and the subsequent target increases, with
exact time courses dependent on stimulus conditions.


ESTABLISHED


Masker amplitude and duration can influence vibrotactile masking.


ESTABLISHED


Tactile masking can be frequency-selective under controlled conditions;
frequency separation can alter the degree of masking.


ESTABLISHED


Whole-body and local tactile sensitivity depend strongly on frequency,
body site, posture, direction, contact conditions, and stimulus
geometry.


CONTEXT-DEPENDENT


Laboratory contactor experiments do not establish the magnitude or time
course of masking in live room-coupled Amastacia and Ankora performance.


CONTEXT-DEPENDENT


A pre-Ankora Amastacia dip may change auditory perception as well as
mechanical/tactile perception. A live effect cannot automatically be
attributed to touch.


PLAUSIBLE / TESTABLE


A sustained mechanically transmitted Amastacia background may reduce the
bodily salience of some Ankora transients when the two overlap strongly
in a listener-contact pathway.


PLAUSIBLE / TESTABLE


Briefly reducing Amastacia-derived background before selected Ankora
strikes may increase perceived Pulse clarity without increasing Ankora
level.


PLAUSIBLE / TESTABLE


Spectral separation between the mechanically dominant Amastacia
background and Ankora transient may improve bodily Pulse definition in
pathways where the two currently overlap.


SPECULATION


A specific 50–150 ms pre-Pulse dip will prove optimal for KLAAT.


SPECULATION


Tactile negative space will always increase activation. It may improve
clarity without increasing activation, or it may become mannered and
predictable.


UNSUPPORTED


More vibration always creates a stronger bodily Pulse.


UNSUPPORTED


A frequency notch based only on Amastacia’s nominal C# will necessarily
improve tactile separation.


UNSUPPORTED


Fingertip masking thresholds directly predict floor-, seat-, or
torso-coupled KLAAT perception.


UNSUPPORTED


Any increased Pulse salience after a live Amastacia dip proves a tactile
mechanism unless auditory contrast has been controlled.


10. KLAAT METHODOLOGY UPDATE


KLAAT now needs to distinguish three different kinds of contrast around
Ankora:


AUDITORY CONTRAST — how much Ankora differs from the airborne sound
immediately surrounding it.


MECHANICAL CONTRAST — how much Ankora changes acceleration/motion at the
relevant contact surface relative to the preceding background.


PERCEPTUAL CONTRAST — how strongly the listener experiences Ankora as a
distinct bodily event.


These should not be collapsed.


The new rule is:


DO NOT MAXIMIZE THE BACKGROUND AND THEN ASK WHY THE PULSE HAS NO EDGE.


A second rule follows:


SUBTRACTION CAN BE AN ACTIVATION TOOL.


That does not mean every Pulse should be preceded by a dip. If the
technique becomes predictable, Session 51 tells us that its perceptual
meaning may change. The eventual composition system will likely need
both continuity and selective contrast.


11. PRACTICAL EXPERIMENT — KRX-052: THE TACTILE NEGATIVE-SPACE TEST


Use one fixed listener position and one standardized posture/contact
condition. Choose a short Amastacia + Ankora passage in which Amastacia
creates a stable field and Ankora repeats at a controlled strike level.


Prepare four conditions:


A — CONTINUOUS FIELD Amastacia remains unchanged through every Ankora
strike.


B — SHORT OPENING Reduce Amastacia for approximately 50 ms immediately
before selected Ankora strikes, returning to the prior level at or just
after the strike.


C — LONGER OPENING Use the same passage with an approximately 150 ms
pre-strike reduction.


D — CONTROL DIP Create the same Amastacia reduction at moments not
followed by an Ankora strike.


Keep Ankora level and timing as constant as practical. Randomize
condition order.


Record airborne audio. If available, record acceleration at the
listener’s actual contact surface. The first goal is not laboratory
metrology; it is to establish whether the manipulation physically
changes the background in the intended direction and whether Ankora
itself remains comparable.


After each condition, collect separate ratings for:


Pulse clarity; bodily impact; activation; perceived Ankora loudness;
noticeability of the Amastacia dip; and whether the Pulse felt more
separate from the Drone.


The critical comparison is A versus B/C.


If B or C increases bodily Pulse clarity while Ankora’s measured level
remains essentially unchanged, tactile-negative-space remains viable.


But then run the harder follow-up: reproduce the airborne loudness
contour while mechanically reducing the contact vibration less, or vice
versa, as equipment allows. That is how KLAAT begins separating auditory
contrast from tactile contrast.


D is important because a pre-Pulse dip can itself become an expectancy
cue. If listeners react strongly to control dips with no drum event,
part of the effect may be prediction rather than masking relief.


A useful result does not require one condition to “win.”


The experiment is trying to discover which mechanism KLAAT is actually
manipulating.

The stronger next unresolved KLAAT question is now compositional:


HOW SHOULD AMASTACIA AND ANKORA TRADE FIGURE AND FIELD ACROSS TIME
WITHOUT THE CONTRAST TECHNIQUE ITSELF BECOMING PREDICTABLE?


That connects Session 51’s expectation model with Session 52’s
tactile-contrast model and points toward an actual KLAAT composition
grammar rather than another equipment lesson.


A future numbered session should proceed there only if the hourly
research produces a genuinely new result. Engineering validation work
should continue underneath the curriculum as methodology, not become the
curriculum itself.
