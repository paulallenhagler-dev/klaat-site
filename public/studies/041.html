AUDITORY MASKING: AMASTACIA CAN MAKE SOUNDS DISAPPEAR

This early session is an archival reconstruction, not a verbatim transcript. Its status is preserved exactly as marked in the source corpus.

Core lesson Masking depends on the overlap of sounds within cochlear auditory filters. Strong low-frequency energy can under many conditions mask higher-frequency components asymmetrically, often described as an upward spread of masking.

Amastacia can therefore act as a spectral gatekeeper. A change in its timbre can hide or reveal components of Ankora without changing the drum itself.

Evidence boundary Established: masking is level- and frequency-dependent; cochlear filters shape spectral competition. Plausible: KLAAT can compose “hidden patterns” that emerge as masking decreases.

KLAAT test Use the same ten Ankora strikes beneath three Amastacia conditions: soft drone, strong dense drone, and strong drone with a different harmonic balance. Ask whether the drum itself seemed to change.
