Core lesson Baroreceptors in the carotid sinus and aortic arch contribute to short-term blood-pressure regulation through a negative-feedback loop. Breathing perturbs this cardiovascular system, and slow respiration can interact strongly with baroreflex dynamics.

Around 0.1 Hz—roughly one cycle every ten seconds—many people show prominent cardiorespiratory oscillations, but the exact resonance varies individually.

At ~137 BPM, approximately 23 Ankora beats occupy about ten seconds. That numerical relationship is interesting as a possible compositional scaffold, not evidence of a healing frequency.

Evidence boundary Established: baroreflex mechanisms regulate short-term blood pressure and interact with respiration. Supported but individual: slow breathing near 0.1 Hz often increases cardiorespiratory oscillation amplitude. Unsupported: assuming 23 Ankora beats “activate the baroreflex” in a therapeutic sense.

KLAAT test Without coaching the listener to breathe at a target rate, count Ankora beats across ten spontaneous breaths and see whether stable values around roughly 20–25 beats per breath emerge.
