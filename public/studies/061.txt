KLAAT Resonance Research — Session 61
THE ROOM IS NOT ONE ROOM: Spatial Resonance Fields, Listener Zones, and the Venue as a Distributed Instrument


Session 60 established that the listener and occupied support form a coupled mechanical system. The strongest unresolved direction in the subsequent hourly work is the airborne counterpart: KLAAT has repeatedly spoken about “the room,” but at low frequencies there is no guarantee that one listener position experiences the same Amastacia field as another.


Room modes, standing-wave nodes and antinodes, source position, seating geometry, occupancy, and support structure can make a venue spatially heterogeneous. One microphone at the center cannot automatically characterize what the audience receives.


THE VENUE IS NOT A SINGLE RESPONSE. IT IS A FIELD OF DIFFERENT LISTENER CONDITIONS.


1. THE ONE-MICROPHONE FALLACY
A calibrated microphone describes its point, not the whole audience. Low-frequency standing waves produce spatial maxima and minima. ONE SPL NUMBER IS NOT THE KLAAT VENUE.


2. AMASTACIA IS ESPECIALLY VULNERABLE TO SPATIAL NON-UNIFORMITY
Amastacia’s sustained C# Drone and harmonics can establish stable low-frequency interference patterns. Different positions may receive different spectral balances even with identical performance.


3. ACOUSTIC NULL DOES NOT MEAN SOMATIC NULL
Airborne pressure and support vibration are different channels. Listener positions can have different AUDIO–TACTILE BALANCE fields.


4. ANKORA OCCUPIES THE FIELD DIFFERENTLY
Ankora’s broadband transients interrogate many room frequencies at once. Her perceptual anchoring can remain different from Amastacia’s sustained spatial coloration.


5. THE LISTENER ZONE
LISTENER ZONE = a bounded audience region whose measured airborne and mechanical characteristics are sufficiently similar for the current KLAAT question.


6. THREE VENUE OUTPUTS
Preserve CENTRAL TENDENCY, SPATIAL SPREAD, and EXTREMA/GRADIENT ZONES. Do not let averaging hide important nulls or peaks.


7. ADAPTIVE MAPPING
Use coarse map → identify gradients/extrema → refine locally → confirm. The goal is the smallest map preserving KLAAT-relevant features.


8. GROUND ANCHOR
Use Amastacia to establish the venue field. Participant/listener coordinates become required research metadata.


9. THRESHOLD GATE
Distinguish COMPOSITIONAL GATE, RECEIVED GATE, and PERCEIVED GATE. A Gate may not be equally strong at every audience position.


10. RISING PULSE
Test SPATIAL ROBUSTNESS: does the KLAAT state remain recognizable across listener zones despite reasonable acoustic variation?


11. RETURN PATH
As Ankora recedes, Amastacia and room decay expose spatial differences again. Return Path can be a diagnostic phase for venue heterogeneity.


12. OCCUPANCY
EMPTY ROOM ≠ AUTOMATICALLY OCCUPIED ROOM. Preserve seating/occupancy state and confirm representative positions under performance conditions.


13. MERGE SESSIONS 60 + 61
Future Venue Resonance Map should pair AIRBORNE SPECTRUM, AIRBORNE LEVEL, POSITION-DEPENDENT DECAY, LOADED-INTERFACE VIBRATION, AUDIO–TACTILE BALANCE, and LISTENER RATING by position.


14. ESTABLISHED EVIDENCE
Low-frequency room response is spatially non-uniform. Standing-wave nodes/antinodes produce position-dependent differences. Low-frequency localization and decay estimates can vary with position. Seating can alter low-frequency propagation. Multi-position methods better represent listening areas than one assumed transfer function. Strategic/adaptive spatial sampling can improve efficiency when validated.


15. CONTEXT-DEPENDENT EVIDENCE
Exact KLAAT variation depends on venue, source, spectra, seating and occupancy. Pure-tone and concert-hall findings do not directly predict a small KLAAT venue. No universal microphone-count rule exists.


16. PLAUSIBLE / TESTABLE HYPOTHESES
Joint airborne + loaded-interface maps will outperform one central SPL reading for predicting bodily salience/audio–tactile unity. Amastacia modal minima may alter Drone fullness. Some positions may be mechanically weighted despite weak airborne low-frequency energy. Gate strength may vary spatially. Adaptive mapping may capture important heterogeneity efficiently.


17. SPECULATION
KLAAT may eventually classify venues by spatial resonance topology, exploit useful listener zones, and choose source placement for architectural robustness rather than one-seat maximum level.


18. UNSUPPORTED CLAIMS
One microphone represents the audience; loudest low-frequency seat is best; antinodes activate the nervous system; acoustic null means no bodily vibration; unusual seats are therapeutic; fixed 5/9/12-point grids always suffice; empty-room maps exactly predict occupied ritual.


19. METHODOLOGY UPDATE
Add AIRBORNE SPATIAL FIELD as a formal KRX layer. Preserve source/listener coordinates, mic height, seating, occupancy, repeatable mapping signal, live condition, frequency-resolved response, spatial variance, extrema/gradient zones and paired mechanics where relevant.
Rules: THE ROOM IS A FIELD, NOT A NUMBER. DO NOT LET AVERAGING ERASE EXTREMA. MAP AIRBORNE AND MECHANICAL CHANNELS SEPARATELY BEFORE COMBINING. LISTENER POSITION IS PART OF THE EXPERIMENT. JUDGE PERFORMANCE FOR SPATIAL ROBUSTNESS, NOT PERFECT SEAT-TO-SEAT IDENTITY.


20. KRX-061 — THE VENUE RESONANCE FIELD TEST
Measure Amastacia spectrum and predefine relevant bands. Build a dense validation grid in one representative indoor venue. Derive frequency-resolved response and identify typical, peak, null and high-gradient zones. Add loaded-interface acceleration at representative positions. Run blinded listener comparisons for Drone fullness, Ankora clarity, bodily salience, audio–tactile unity, Threshold Gate strength and overall coherence. Retrospectively compare smaller 5-zone, 9-zone, perimeter+center and adaptive sampling schemes against the dense reference.


Primary question: WHAT IS THE SMALLEST PRACTICAL VENUE-MAPPING SCHEME THAT PRESERVES THE SPATIAL FEATURES THAT ACTUALLY MATTER TO KLAAT?
