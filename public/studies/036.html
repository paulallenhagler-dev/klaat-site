Core lesson The brain compensates for differences in sensory transmission and processing time so that related auditory and tactile signals can be experienced as one event. This occurs within a temporal binding window whose width depends on stimulus and context.

Sound itself takes time to travel: roughly 9 ms over 3 m, 29 ms over 10 m, and 58 ms over 20 m. Mechanical vibration may travel through structures by a different path and speed.

Evidence boundary Established: multisensory temporal binding is real and timing-dependent. No single binding-window number applies universally. Plausible: live KLAAT can create stronger auditory-tactile unity than a recording lacking mechanical coupling.

KLAAT test Play a steady Ankora pattern for about one minute and omit one expected strike. Ask whether the missing event produced a physical sense of absence even though nothing struck the body.
