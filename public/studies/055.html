THE DIVISION OF LABOR: Hearing Carries the Clock, Touch Gives the Pulse a Body

KLAAT's strongest current working hypothesis is therefore:

SESSION 055 / 164Pulse & RhythmRESEARCH SESSION

Session 54 defined Threshold Gate as controlled model replacement. The strongest new hourly finding changes another part of KLAAT: Ankora's audible strike and felt vibration should not be assumed to perform the same temporal job.

A 2025 Journal of Neuroscience experiment comparing the same rhythmic structure in audition and touch found stronger beat-related low-frequency periodization and more stable tapping for auditory rhythm. Tactile stimulation robustly represented individual events but did not show the same significant higher-order beat-related organization in that paradigm. Broader multisensory work still supports tactile rhythm perception and audiotactile interaction. The correct conclusion is modality asymmetry, not tactile incapacity.

KLAAT's strongest current working hypothesis is therefore:

HEARING CARRIES THE CLOCK. TOUCH GIVES SELECTED EVENTS A BODY.

1. ONE STRIKE, SEVERAL PERCEPTUAL JOBS

An Ankora strike reaches a listener through airborne sound, structure-borne vibration, room reflections, body contact and sometimes vision. Those channels need not perform the same computation.

Audition has unusually strong temporal resolution and a well-established role in beat perception and sensorimotor synchronization. Touch can represent rhythm and timing, but current evidence does not justify assuming that felt vibration independently constructs the same hierarchical beat representation with equal strength.

The useful KLAAT question becomes:

NOT "HOW MUCH AUDIO + VIBRATION?" BUT "WHAT JOB IS EACH CHANNEL DOING?"

2. ANKORA MAY CONTAIN A CLOCK AND AN IMPACT LAYER

Provisional decomposition:

AUDITORY ANKORA — temporal scaffold, beat prediction, omission expectation, synchronization cue.

TACTILE ANKORA — bodily localization, impact, event salience, physical consequence, and possibly stronger common-event unity when it agrees with the sound.

These roles can overlap. They are not mutually exclusive. But separating them yields falsifiable predictions.

If audible timing is already clear, increasing tactile energy may greatly increase bodily impact while barely changing beat-continuation accuracy. If tactile vibration is presented without a strong auditory scaffold, listeners may detect the rhythm yet maintain a less stable higher-order beat. If tactile emphasis is concentrated on metrically important events rather than every subdivision, KLAAT may preserve a clear auditory clock while making selected moments disproportionately physical.

3. "ENTRAINMENT" NEEDS A HARDER DEFINITION

At approximately 137 BPM, Ankora's provisional calibration Pulse is about 2.28 beats per second, with an inter-beat interval near 438 ms. That lies comfortably inside ordinary human beat-perception and synchronization territory.

That makes it useful. It does not make 137 BPM biologically magical.

KLAAT should reserve entrainment for specified phenomena:

TEMPORAL-ATTENTIONAL ENTRAINMENT — attention becomes predictively organized around recurring event times. SENSORIMOTOR SYNCHRONIZATION — movement becomes phase-related to the Pulse. AUDITORY NEURAL TRACKING — measured neural activity contains stimulus- or beat-related periodic structure under an appropriate analysis. INTERPERSONAL SYNCHRONIZATION — people coordinate timing with one another.

None automatically means global brain synchronization, a forced 2.28-Hz brain state, therapeutic brainwave entrainment, or whole-body neural locking.

4. TOUCH MAY MAKE A BEAT CONSEQUENTIAL WITHOUT MAKING IT MORE BEAT-LIKE

Imagine two passages with identical audible Ankora timing. In A, little structure-borne vibration reaches the listener. In B, selected strikes are strongly coupled into the support.

B may feel more embodied, consequential and physically real. But if both conditions produce equally accurate continuation tapping after Ankora stops, tactile augmentation strengthened embodiment without strengthening the internal beat representation.

That is not failure. It maps the tactile channel's actual role.

5. CROSSMODAL CAUSAL COHERENCE MAY BE THE BRIDGE

Several hourly passes converge on another idea: audiotactile integration is not simply sound plus touch. Perception depends partly on whether signals are plausible consequences of one common physical event. Timing, spatial correspondence, prior exposure and cue reliability can matter.

KLAAT's working construct is:

CROSSMODAL CAUSAL COHERENCE — the degree to which heard and felt components are perceived as belonging to the same physical cause.

This is not yet a proven KLAAT mechanism. But it is a better hypothesis than "more vibration = more embodiment."

A powerful floor vibration that arrives late, feels spatially disconnected, or has an envelope unrelated to Ankora may add energy while weakening event unity. A smaller tactile event with plausible timing and location may feel more convincingly like the body-component of Ankora.

IMPROVE THE ORGANIZATION OF THE CHANNELS BEFORE INCREASING THEIR ENERGY.

6. PERFECT PHYSICAL SIMULTANEITY IS NOT PERCEPTUAL UNITY

Audiotactile perception has finite timing tolerance. Laboratory work shows that sound and touch need not be physically simultaneous to interact, and sustained exposure to a stable crossmodal delay can shift perceived simultaneity.

Live KLAAT has multiple propagation paths. Airborne Ankora travels through air. Mechanical transients travel through drum support, floor, furniture and body. Each path has its own filtering, onset, rise, decay and latency.

So "generated at the same instant" does not guarantee "received as one event." Literal zero measured delay is not necessarily the perceptual optimum either. The relevant variable is listener- and setup-specific alignment.

7. AMASTACIA'S ROLE: THE CONTINUOUS WORLD

The modality-weighted model clarifies Amastacia.

She does not need to compete with Ankora for temporal dominance. Her strongest role may be maintaining the continuous spectral, spatial, respiratory and mechanical world inside which Ankora's discrete clock becomes meaningful.

Auditorily, Amastacia supplies sustained context against which transient Ankora onsets become distinct. Mechanically, she may create a continuous bodily field against which selected Ankora events become figures. Perceptually, she can preserve identity across state changes.

AMASTACIA DEFINES THE WORLD. ANKORA DEFINES WHEN. TOUCH HELPS DECIDE WHICH MOMENTS BECOME PHYSICALLY CONSEQUENTIAL.

8. THE FOUR-PHASE ARCHITECTURE

GROUND ANCHOR — ESTABLISH THE WORLD AND BODY MAP Amastacia establishes continuous acoustic identity and, where coupling permits, a stable somatic background. The listener learns the room, Drone, body-contact field and source relationship.

THRESHOLD GATE — INTRODUCE A CLOCK WITH A BODY Ankora's audible transient establishes or changes temporal grammar. Its felt component makes selected temporal events physically consequential. A strong Gate may be the first unmistakable moment when the new auditory clock and bodily event become causally coherent.

RISING PULSE — SEPARATE TEMPORAL DENSITY FROM TACTILE DENSITY The audible Ankora pattern can become dense while tactile emphasis remains sparse. Strong tactile coupling can be reserved for metrically important beats, rule changes, omissions followed by returns, or other consequential events.

KLAAT gains two independently controllable trajectories: AUDITORY TEMPORAL DENSITY and TACTILE CONSEQUENCE DENSITY.

RETURN PATH — REMOVE THE BODY BEFORE OR AFTER THE CLOCK TACTILE WITHDRAWAL FIRST: the clock persists but loses bodily consequence. AUDITORY PULSE WITHDRAWAL FIRST: explicit timing disappears while residual room/body vibration remains. These may produce different endings and should be tested.

9. SAFETY AND LANGUAGE BOUNDARY: FELT LOW FREQUENCY IS NOT VESTIBULAR ACTIVATION

Vestibular otolith organs can respond to sufficiently intense sound/vibration, which underlies VEMP testing. But that does not establish routine vestibular activation by ordinary musical low-frequency exposure.

At sane KLAAT performance levels, the more defensible default model is: AUDITORY LOW-FREQUENCY PERCEPTION + CUTANEOUS/SOMATIC VIBRATION + STRUCTURE-BORNE COUPLING + ATTENTION/EXPECTATION.

If dizziness or disequilibrium occurs, treat it as a safety-relevant listener response, not proof that KLAAT successfully engaged a desired vestibular mechanism. Do not chase vestibular effects by increasing SPL.

10. PHYSICAL VIBRATION IS NOT PERCEIVED VIBRATION

Equal RMS acceleration at different frequencies, axes, contact areas and body interfaces does not imply equal perceived intensity. Whole-body thresholds vary with frequency and axis; spatial summation and contact geometry matter.

KLAAT therefore needs two somatic maps:

PHYSICAL SOMATIC TRANSFER MAP — calibrated, unweighted, axis-resolved mechanical measurements.

PERCEPTUAL SOMATIC MAP — empirically established detection, equal-sensation, bodily-salience and localization measures under defined posture/contact conditions.

Never rename the first map as the second. The location with the largest accelerometer RMS is not automatically where KLAAT is felt most.

11. EVIDENCE BOUNDARY

ESTABLISHED: Humans can perceive rhythm through audition and touch, but the modalities need not produce equivalent higher-order beat representations.

ESTABLISHED: In a 2025 controlled comparison, auditory presentation produced stronger beat-related periodization and more stable tapping than tactile presentation; tactile responses tracked individual events more strongly and did not show the same significant beat-related organization in that paradigm.

ESTABLISHED: Auditory rhythm supports predictive beat perception and sensorimotor synchronization across a broad tempo range. ~137 BPM is comfortably within ordinary human beat/synchronization ranges.

ESTABLISHED: Audiotactile interaction depends on temporal and spatial relationships; repeated exposure can recalibrate perceived crossmodal timing under controlled conditions.

ESTABLISHED: Whole-body/tactile sensitivity varies with frequency, axis, body site and interface. Equal raw acceleration does not guarantee equal sensation.

ESTABLISHED: Vestibular organs can respond to sufficiently intense sound/vibration, but this does not establish routine vestibular activation by ordinary musical low-frequency exposure.

CONTEXT-DEPENDENT: The auditory-versus-tactile beat result used controlled stimulation, not live whole-body Ankora vibration. It supports modality asymmetry, not tactile incapacity.

CONTEXT-DEPENDENT: Audiotactile binding windows, preferred delays, spatial tolerances and recalibration time courses are stimulus-, task-, listener- and setup-dependent.

CONTEXT-DEPENDENT: Laboratory vibration thresholds and ISO-style weightings cannot be imported directly as a KLAAT felt-intensity function.

PLAUSIBLE/TESTABLE: In normal-hearing KLAAT listeners, audible Ankora provides the primary beat scaffold while mechanically felt Ankora contributes more strongly to bodily impact, localization, salience and event unity.

PLAUSIBLE/TESTABLE: Concentrating tactile emphasis on structurally important Ankora events may increase bodily impact without requiring every subdivision to be equally vibratory.

PLAUSIBLE/TESTABLE: Audiotactile common-source/unity judgments may predict embodied impact better than SPL or acceleration alone.

PLAUSIBLE/TESTABLE: Ground Anchor exposure may recalibrate a listener toward a stable venue-specific audio–support timing relationship.

SPECULATION: KLAAT may work better when modalities are assigned complementary compositional jobs rather than made maximally redundant.

SPECULATION: Rising Pulse may benefit from increasing auditory density while keeping tactile consequence sparse enough to preserve bodily contrast.

UNSUPPORTED: Whole-body vibration independently entrains the brain to Ankora's beat. UNSUPPORTED: Auditory and tactile entrainment are neurologically equivalent. UNSUPPORTED: 137 BPM is a therapeutic, shamanic or biologically privileged frequency. UNSUPPORTED: A 2.28-Hz Pulse forces the brain into a 2.28-Hz state. UNSUPPORTED: More floor vibration necessarily strengthens beat perception or embodiment. UNSUPPORTED: Felt bass, pressure, sway or dizziness proves vestibular activation.

12. KLAAT METHODOLOGY UPDATE

From Session 55 onward, Ankora experiments should separate: BEAT ORGANIZATION — prediction, continuation accuracy, tapping phase consistency. BODILY IMPACT — how physically consequential the event feels. EVENT UNITY — whether heard and felt components seem like one cause. TACTILE SALIENCE/LOCATION — strength and body location of felt vibration.

Audiotactile records should preserve airborne onset/envelope, support-acceleration onset/envelope, relative delay, spatial source/contact geometry, axis-resolved vibration, posture/contact condition and exposure history.

New rules: DO NOT MAKE TOUCH PROVE THE CLOCK IF HEARING ALREADY DOES IT BETTER. DO NOT CONFUSE A STRONGER BODY EVENT WITH A STRONGER INTERNAL BEAT. MEASURE CROSSMODAL UNITY BEFORE ADDING MORE ENERGY. FELT LOW FREQUENCY IS NOT SYNONYMOUS WITH VESTIBULAR STIMULATION.

13. KRX-055 — THE CLOCK-AND-BODY TEST

Create one simple Ankora rhythm around the current ~137 BPM reference and reproduce it in four conditions.

A — AUDITORY ONLY Clear audible Ankora Pulse with mechanical transmission minimized as far as practical.

B — TACTILE ONLY Deliver the corresponding measured tactile rhythm through a controlled support while masking or removing the audible Pulse as far as practical.

C — CONGRUENT AUDITORY + TACTILE Use the same audible Pulse plus tactile events aligned to every principal beat.

D — METRIC-SELECTIVE TACTILE Keep the audible rhythm identical to C, but concentrate comparable tactile emphasis on structurally important beats rather than making every event equally physical.

Hold airborne level/envelope constant across auditory conditions. Quantify interface acceleration in tactile conditions. Keep posture/contact geometry constant.

Measure: BEAT — tapping phase consistency, asynchrony and 8–12 continuation taps after unexpected Pulse removal. BODY — bodily impact and tactile salience. UNITY — whether heard/felt components seemed like one event/source. EXPECTATION — response to occasional silent omissions.

If A, C and D produce similar continuation accuracy while C/D produce greater bodily impact than A, the division-of-labor hypothesis gains support.

If B produces continuation performance comparable to auditory conditions, the auditory-dominance hypothesis weakens for KLAAT's tactile stimulation.

If D produces equal or stronger bodily impact than C with fewer strongly tactile events, KLAAT gains a compositional tool: tactile consequence can be allocated selectively instead of saturating every Pulse.

If C strongly outperforms A on beat continuation as well as embodiment, touch contributes more directly to temporal organization than the current model predicts.

RECOMMENDED NEXT DIRECTION

The highest-value unresolved direction is CROSSMODAL CAUSAL COHERENCE.

If hearing carries much of the temporal scaffold while touch gives selected events bodily consequence, the next question is:

WHAT MAKES THE HEARD ANKORA AND FELT ANKORA BECOME ONE PHYSICAL EVENT IN THE LISTENER'S MODEL?

The hourly work identifies timing, spatial correspondence, cue reliability and exposure history as candidate variables. Session 49 established timing fidelity as a candidate control variable; the next step is to test whether timing and space jointly influence a higher-order common-cause judgment, and whether that judgment predicts bodily impact better than either physical variable alone.

That should become the next numbered session only if continuing research survives the relevance/novelty gate.
