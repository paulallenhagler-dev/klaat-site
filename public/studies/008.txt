SESSION 8 — THE ROOM IS PART OF THE INSTRUMENT
ARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT VERBATIM
Core lesson
Rooms have resonant modes and standing-wave patterns. A simple axial-mode estimate is f = c/(2L); a 12-foot room dimension gives a first mode around the high-40-Hz range. Because low-frequency wavelengths are comparable to room dimensions, a few feet of listener movement can produce large differences in bass level and spectral balance.


Amastacia therefore excites not only the air but the geometry of the venue. The same performance can feel radically different at different audience positions.


Evidence boundary
Established: enclosed spaces produce modal reinforcement and cancellation; reverberation and position alter perceived sound. Unsupported: treating a room mode as a biological “healing resonance.”


KLAAT test
Hold one stable Amastacia configuration and map at least six positions in the room. Rate loudness, bass weight, overtone clarity, and bodily vibration separately.
