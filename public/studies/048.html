REPRODUCIBLE ACOUSTIC MAPPING: WHEN A KLAAT MEASUREMENT BECOMES EVIDENCE

POSITION IS PART OF THE RESULT

SESSION 048 / 164Acoustics & SpaceRESEARCH SESSION

KLAAT now has a hearing-exposure principle: compose perceived intensity while managing acoustic dose. But Session 47 exposed the next problem immediately. A number on a sound meter is not automatically evidence. If microphone position, duration, weighting, calibration state, or measurement-chain capability is unknown, two “dB readings” may not be comparable.

THE CENTRAL RULE No formal KLAAT acoustic measurement should exist without POSITION + DURATION + METRIC + CALIBRATION STATUS + PERFORMANCE PHASE. For research-grade work, add MEASUREMENT-CHAIN CAPABILITY.

POSITION IS PART OF THE RESULT Rooms are spatially nonuniform. Low-frequency modes create peaks and nulls; reflections alter spectra and decay; source direction and distance change received level. WHO’s safe-listening standard likewise defines a reference measurement position and notes that listeners closer to or farther from loudspeakers/stage can receive different levels. One reference point therefore cannot imply uniform exposure throughout a KLAAT room.

KLAAT POSITION MAP P1 — Performer ear: Paul’s hearing zone, representing repeated rehearsal, soundcheck, recording and performance exposure. P2 — Nearest intended listener: closest expected audience position. P3 — Central listener: representative room-center audience position. P4 — Rear listener: representative farther position. P5 — Hotspot, optional: position found during an exploratory sweep to be unusually strong. These labels describe roles, not fixed distances. Every record still logs actual distance, height and orientation.

DURATION MUST MATCH THE CLAIM LAeq,T integrates acoustic energy over a stated interval T. LAeq,30s and LAeq,15min answer different questions. KLAAT therefore needs nested time scales: whole ritual, individual phase, moving window where relevant, and transient metrics for Ankora. A short Threshold Gate may be the loudest moment while sustained Amastacia contributes more cumulative energy because it lasts longer.

CALIBRATION IS NOT VALIDATION An acoustical calibrator checks/adjusts level against a known reference. NIOSH recommends calibration before and after formal noise measurements. But passing a calibration check does not prove accurate response across every frequency Amastacia produces, prove that Ankora peaks cannot overload the input, or validate an FFT/octave-band display merely because broadband LAeq is accurate.

Every KLAAT measurement chain must answer four separate questions: Is LEVEL calibrated? Is the relevant FREQUENCY RANGE validated? Can it capture the expected PEAK without clipping? Is the requested METRIC supported and validated on that chain? “Calibrated” answers only the first.

THREE EVIDENCE TIERS Tier E — Exploratory: convenient phone/internal-microphone or other incompletely documented chain. Useful for procedure development and rough within-session comparisons, not strong exposure or spectral claims. Tier R — Research-grade exploratory: calibrated external microphone with documented hardware, calibration, level range and relevant validation. Suitable for serious internal comparison only within demonstrated capability. Tier F — Formal: professional standards-conforming chain with documentation appropriate to the claim, correct calibrator, documented settings and controlled procedure.

NIOSH reports that its iOS Sound Level Meter app is approximately ±2 dBA under stated validation conditions and, with a calibrated external microphone, meets tested Type/Class 2 requirements of IEC 61672-3 periodic testing. KLAAT must not inflate that into a claim that every iPhone/external-microphone combination is a fully Class 2 meter. Formal records should include STANDARD CLAIM SCOPE: none / laboratory validation / IEC 61672-3 periodic-test scope / documented full conformity appropriate to the claim.

MEASUREMENT CHAIN CAPABILITY SHEET Before equipment becomes a KLAAT standard kit, record recorder/phone model; app/software/version; microphone make/model/serial; calibrator and reference level; calibration/check date; documented frequency response/bandwidth; validated level range; maximum unclipped level/overload information; supported A/C/Z weightings; supported LAeq/max/peak statistics; whether octave/third-octave or FFT output has independent validation; standards-claim scope; and known limitations. If the microphone changes, treat it as a new chain.

STANDARDIZE THE PERFORMANCE A room comparison is weak if Paul plays different material in each room. KLAAT therefore needs a Standard Mapping Passage: a short calibration composition, not a performance piece. Initial version: 2:00 Ground Anchor — neutral Amastacia; 1:30 Threshold Gate — predetermined transition; 3:00 Rising Pulse — Amastacia + Ankora at controlled dynamics and a logged reference pulse; 1:30 Return Path — systematic subtraction. The ~137 BPM Sample Zero pulse may be a provisional reference until formally measured.

WHAT TO RECORD At minimum: whole-passage LAeq; phase-specific LAeq; duration; supported peak/max metric; position/microphone height; source distance/orientation; room/floor type; instrument configuration; performance notes; evidence tier; calibration status. Session 49 will address A/C/Z weighting and spectral measurement; Session 48 is about making the exposure channel reproducible.

KEEP THREE CHANNELS SEPARATE Acoustic channel: microphone → airborne pressure → LAeq/peak/spectrum. Mechanical channel: accelerometer → structure motion → acceleration/frequency data. Subjective channel: listener → bodily intensity, activation, annoyance/discomfort, spatial immersion. The performance may seek fusion; the experiment seeks separation. Only after separate measurement should KLAAT test how the channels combine perceptually.

THE FOUR PHASES BECOME EXPERIMENTAL EPOCHS Ground Anchor asks how much acoustic energy is required to establish the Drone convincingly. Threshold Gate becomes a contrast-efficiency problem: perceived transition versus acoustic-level change. Rising Pulse becomes the activation-per-dB laboratory: can temporal density, attack, roughness, prediction and mechanical coupling raise activation without proportional airborne exposure? Return Path tests subtraction: how rapidly can acoustic energy fall while salience remains high through expectation, contrast and room decay?

DUAL EXPOSURE ACCOUNTING Maintain an Audience Event Record for one ritual and a separate Performer Cumulative Record for Paul across setup, soundcheck, repeated experiments, rehearsal, recording takes and performance. Technical rehearsal should occur at the lowest level that still answers the technical question; full-intensity repetition should be reserved for tests that require it.

EVIDENCE BOUNDARY ESTABLISHED — Sound level depends on position; room geometry, distance, orientation and reflections produce spatial variation. ESTABLISHED — Equivalent-level metrics require a defined averaging interval. ESTABLISHED — NIOSH recommends before/after calibration checks and detailed measurement records for formal noise-exposure work. ESTABLISHED — NIOSH publishes validation data for its iOS SLM app and supports LAeq plus A/C/Z weighting; the tested external-microphone configuration has IEC 61672-3 periodic-test evidence. ESTABLISHED — Single-point calibration does not establish full-spectrum accuracy or transient headroom. CONTEXT-DEPENDENT — Phone/external-microphone systems can be useful, but claims depend on exact hardware, software, bandwidth, level range and validation evidence. PLAUSIBLE/TESTABLE — Phase mapping will show that perceived activation and acoustic energy do not rise in parallel. PLAUSIBLE/TESTABLE — Rehearsal/repeated-take exposure may become a larger cumulative constraint for Paul than a single public performance. SPECULATION — One specific audience position will consistently be the KLAAT hotspot across rooms. UNSUPPORTED — “The meter was calibrated, therefore its low-frequency spectrum is accurate.” UNSUPPORTED — “One audience reading describes everyone’s exposure.”

KLAAT METHODOLOGY ADDITION MEASURE THE CLAIM YOU ARE MAKING. A hearing-exposure claim requires a defensible acoustic-exposure chain. A low-frequency spectral claim requires validated bandwidth/spectral measurement. A transient claim requires peak headroom and temporal capability. A tactile claim requires mechanical measurement. A perceptual claim requires listener data. No single sensor stands in for the whole ritual.

KRX-048 — THE FIRST REPRODUCIBLE KLAAT MAP Choose one room and mark P1 performer-ear, P2 nearest intended listener, P3 center and P4 rear; add P5 only if an exploratory sweep finds an obvious hotspot. Perform the same 8-minute Standard Mapping Passage for each position, or use simultaneous meters if available. Do not deliberately increase intensity between repetitions.

Log the chain and calibration status before the first run; repeat the calibration check after the final run where supported. Record whole-passage LAeq, phase-specific LAeq, supported peak/max metric, exact geometry and evidence tier. Add three neutral listener ratings at each measured position: perceived loudness, bodily intensity and activation, each 0–10. Do not tell the listener which position is expected to be strongest.

Ask four questions: Which position receives the greatest acoustic exposure? Which phase contributes most at each position? Does highest LAeq also mean highest bodily intensity? Does Rising Pulse create a larger increase in activation than in LAeq? If these answers diverge, that divergence is a research result.
