SESSION 7 — SILENCE IS AN ACTIVE STIMULUS
ARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT VERBATIM
Core lesson
Silence after sustained sound is not perceptually equivalent to silence before it. Adaptation, expectation, and contrast make removal itself informative. After continuous drone or pulse, stopping the source can increase awareness of internal sensations, room noise, breathing, and the residual expectation of the next event.


For KLAAT, silence should be composed rather than treated as empty space. Return Path can use abrupt subtraction as a major event.


Evidence boundary
Established: sensory adaptation and predictive processing change how a stimulus is experienced over time; contrast alters salience. Plausible: post-sound silence may increase interoceptive attention in some listeners. Unsupported: interpreting that inward attention as automatic healing or trauma release.


KLAAT test
Play Amastacia continuously for about ten minutes, stop abruptly, and remain silent for three minutes. Ask when the experience felt strongest: during the drone, at the cutoff, or in the silence afterward.
