SESSION 9 — WHEN SOUND BECOMES TOUCH
ARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT VERBATIM
Core lesson
Auditory and tactile perception overlap in real-world low-frequency experiences. Vibrotactile thresholds depend on frequency, body site, coupling, and intensity. The Pacinian system is often especially sensitive in roughly the few-hundred-hertz region under laboratory conditions, but strong lower-frequency vibration can also be felt through other mechanisms and through large contact surfaces.


KLAAT can therefore think in terms of a somatic threshold crossing: auditory → auditory-somatic → somatically dominant.


Evidence boundary
Established: sound and mechanical vibration can be perceived through different but interacting sensory systems. Context-dependent: the exact threshold at which a listener reports bodily sensation varies substantially.


KLAAT test
Increase Amastacia intensity slowly at a fixed position and have the listener mark three points: first clear audibility, first clear bodily sensation, and point where the bodily component becomes a major part of the event.
