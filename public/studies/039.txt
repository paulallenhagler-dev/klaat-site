SESSION 39 — STARTLE REFLEX VS RHYTHMIC ANTICIPATION
ARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT VERBATIM


Core lesson
Activation does not require surprise. The acoustic startle reflex is a rapid involuntary response to sudden intense unexpected stimulation. Orienting is broader attentional redirection. Rhythmic anticipation is preparation for an expected event.


These can produce very different subjective states even when the physical strike is similar.


Repeated startle tends to habituate, while rhythmic prediction can become stronger as the pattern becomes more established.


Evidence boundary
Established: startle, orienting, and temporal anticipation are distinguishable phenomena. Established: context strongly changes response to the same sound. Unsupported: treating a strong bodily reaction as evidence of therapeutic release.


KLAAT test
Present one matched Ankora strike in three contexts: after silence, during continuous Amastacia, and after a long predictable sequence. Rate surprise, physical impact, pre-strike tension, and urge to move separately.
