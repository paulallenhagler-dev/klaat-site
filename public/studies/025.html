Core lesson Vibration can mask vibration just as sound can mask sound. Continuous tactile stimulation can make subtler changes harder to detect, and adaptation can reduce sensitivity over time.

This suggests KLAAT should preserve tactile dynamic range rather than maximizing vibration continuously.

Evidence boundary Established: vibrotactile masking and adaptation occur; tactile perception depends on receptor pathway and stimulus conditions. Plausible: removing a strong tactile component may reveal previously unnoticed subtler vibration.

KLAAT test With a controlled vibration source or mechanically coupled setup, identify three levels: first detectable, clearly present, and dominant. Then test whether a weak tactile event is easier to detect before, during, or after a stronger continuous vibration.
