THE RESPONSE THAT CROSSED TIME ZERO

Why a bodily signal recorded after Amastacia begins may belong to the moments before the sound

SESSION 139 / 164Evidence & MethodRESEARCH SESSION

GOVERNING PRINCIPLE Experience-led creation. Science-informed refinement.

RESEARCH STATUS Critical synthesis of primary psychophysiology and KLAAT's accumulated research notes. No original KLAAT listener physiology, participant responses, sensor recordings, or new acoustic measurements were available. Numerical results below belong to external studies or explicitly hypothetical examples. No claim that KLAAT induces trance, piloerection, autonomic entrainment, dopamine release, or therapeutic benefit is established.

CENTRAL QUESTION

Imagine that a consenting listener hears Paul begin a spontaneous Amastacia-and-Ankora performance. An electrodermal sensor rises three seconds after the first audible C# drone. A researcher marks that rise as an immediate response to KLAAT. But the listener saw Ankora, heard a description of a 'resonance ritual,' and anticipated the first sound several seconds earlier. Could the physiological signal that APPEARED after the first note have been initiated before the first note?

Yes, in principle. The body does not report every sympathetic event at the exact moment it is initiated. The recorded skin-conductance trace is a delayed, temporally extended consequence of sudomotor activity, and responses to adjacent events can overlap. An observed post-onset increase is therefore not sufficient to identify the sound as its cause. The issue is not whether the experience was authentic. It is whether the research assigns its origin to the correct part of the experience.

This session does NOT merely repeat the warning that a goosebump can occur before the music. Its new question is the inverse and more difficult one: WHEN THE MEASURED PHYSIOLOGICAL CHANGE BEGINS AFTER SOUND ONSET, CAN ITS GENERATING EVENT STILL PRECEDE SOUND ONSET? The answer requires a response-latency model, overlapping-event analysis, and a causal-attribution rule that none of the adjacent notes by itself supplies. The methodological synthesis is applicable to an authentic uninterrupted improvisation, not a hypothetical scored ritual.

2. FIVE TIMES THAT MUST NOT BE COLLAPSED

For any future KLAAT listener session, distinguish these timestamps:

T_context: first meaningful context, such as entering the performance space, seeing Amastacia and Ankora, or receiving a verbal description. T_expectation: when the listener starts anticipating an imminent event. This is often latent; it cannot be read directly from a stopwatch. T_audio: the actual first audible sound reaching the listener, not simply the moment Paul moves or a playback button is pressed. T_initiation: when a physiological process begins upstream of the measured sensor output. Usually unobserved without specialized measurements. T_observation: when a sensor or observer detects a change, such as an electrodermal rise or visible piloerection.

A sixth time, T_report, is when a person describes what they felt. That may be minutes later and may reflect memory reconstruction. The ordering T_context < T_audio < T_observation is easy to measure in principle; it does not establish T_audio < T_initiation. This distinction is the heart of the session.

In an ordinary acoustic KLAAT performance, T_audio itself can be ambiguous: Amastacia may begin with a soft breath edge before the stable drone is audible; Ankora may enter after the drone; room reflections and audience position alter the arrival of the first sound. These are small acoustic-timeline issues relative to a twenty-second introduction, but they matter if someone later makes claims about subsecond response timing. Do not treat the first large waveform peak as the only possible acoustic onset.

3. WHAT THE HUMAN SKIN-CONDUCTANCE LITERATURE ACTUALLY SHOWS

Electrodermal activity (EDA) reflects changes in the electrical properties of skin associated with sweat-gland activity. It is commonly used as a peripheral index of sympathetic activation. It is NOT a direct readout of pleasure, fear, trance, attention, or a specific brain rhythm. The same broad physiological channel responds in many situations, including orienting, movement-related events, heat, and emotional arousal.

Benedek and Kaernbach's 2010 Journal of Neuroscience Methods study examined a continuous decomposition of phasic electrodermal activity. The researchers emphasized that conventional skin-conductance responses overlap: one response may rise while an earlier response is still decaying. In their paired-noise experiment, stimuli were separated by 2, 4, 6, or 8 seconds. At a 2-second separation, the averaged raw skin-conductance trace could merge the two responses so thoroughly that the second did not form a distinct local minimum or peak. The modeled phasic driver nevertheless separated responses under the study's assumptions. This is a demonstration of a measurement problem, not proof that the model recovers a unique neural event in every real-world case.

In that experiment, the mean estimated phasic-driver impulse time was 2.30 seconds after a stimulus (median 2.23; SD 0.48), with an average impulse beginning around 1.38 seconds and ending around 3.23 seconds under a symmetric-shape approximation. The authors' predefined 1–4-second response window captured nearly 92% of the average phasic-driver response area within the first five seconds. These numbers are tied to brief, controlled noise stimuli and their analysis model. They are NOT KLAAT response latencies, nor do they imply that every EDA rise at three seconds is attributable to the event at zero.

The same paper reported that conventional trough-to-peak scoring underestimated modeled response amplitudes relative to an improved method by 49% on the study's transformed scale overall; at the shortest 2-second interval, the relative discrepancy was greater. These quantitative differences show that apparent response magnitude depends partly on how overlapping responses are scored. They do not demonstrate an improvement in the listener's underlying state, and a numerical algorithm is not a substitute for knowing which event occurred when.

Benedek and Kaernbach's companion 2010 Psychophysiology paper used nonnegative deconvolution to separate tonic and phasic components and estimate a compact driver. The approach is useful precisely because a measured skin-conductance curve is not an instantaneous copy of the underlying event. Yet its validity depends on response-shape assumptions, signal quality, and the appropriateness of its physiological model. The more densely events occur, the more important it is to test model sensitivity rather than select whichever output appears most dramatic.

4. THE PRE-AUDIO OBSERVATION THAT MAKES THE PROBLEM CONCRETE

In a different 2011 experiment, Benedek and Kaernbach measured visible forearm piloerection during emotionally salient audio excerpts from music and films. Their trial sequence included a 30-second rest baseline, a 20-second written description, then audio presentation. Among 400 trials from 50 participants, visible piloerection occurred in 70 trials (17.5%). The hourly note reports that the first visible onset occurred during the description period in 13% of piloerection-positive trials. That denominator is the 70 positive trials, not all 400 trials, and the exact rounded count should not be reconstructed as a new precision claim.

This finding does not mean that the description caused the piloerection. Participants could have anticipated the excerpt, carried over activation from an earlier trial, reacted to their own thoughts, or changed physiologically for other reasons. It DOES establish that a research protocol's nominal 'stimulus period' does not contain every event that matters to the listener. It also shows why a pre-audio baseline that includes evocative instructions may already be an activated baseline rather than a neutral one.

The 2011 study found that piloerection was associated with changes in phasic EDA and respiration depth compared with matched no-piloerection trials. It did not establish that all subjective chills are visible goosebumps or that the same response identifies one particular emotion. Grewe and colleagues separately observed chills in response to acoustic, visual, tactile, gustatory, and internally generated experiences among 36 participants, with different emotional interpretations across modalities. A visible bodily event can be genuine without uniquely identifying what caused it or how it felt.

5. A HYPOTHETICAL KLAAT TRACE: THE FALSE POST-ONSET EFFECT

The following times and amplitudes are invented solely to demonstrate the logic. They are NOT actual KLAAT recordings or participant measurements.

At t = -15 s, a listener is told that an acoustic resonance ritual is about to begin. At t = -3 s, the listener sees Paul raise Ankora and prepare Amastacia. At t = 0 s, the first audible Amastacia sound reaches the listener. At t = +2.5 s, a skin-conductance trace begins rising. At t = +5 s, the trace reaches a local peak.

An analyst who only knows the audio file's zero point may attribute the rise to Amastacia. Yet a hypothetical sympathetic event initiated near t = -1 s, in response to the visible preparation or expectation, could produce a delayed skin-conductance change after t = 0. A different event initiated by the actual sound might also contribute. A third spontaneous event could be unrelated to either. The recorded curve alone cannot decide among them.

If two such events occur close together, the sensor can register a combined response. Even a sophisticated decomposition algorithm can only estimate latent impulses under assumptions; it cannot infer an unlogged introduction, visual cue, or participant thought from skin conductance alone. The physiological signal is a response channel, not a causal history recorder.

6. THE MODEL: RESPONSE KERNEL, NOT MUSICAL PHASE

A useful conceptual model is:

Observed EDA(t) = tonic baseline(t) + sum of event-linked response shapes(t - event time) + movement/environmental noise(t).

In signal-processing language, each initiating event is blurred by a physiological response kernel. The kernel has a rise and decay, so two events separated by only a few seconds may yield one broad observed wave. A simplified convolution model can express this as observed phasic EDA = latent sympathetic drive convolved with a response function, plus noise. The kernel is a model of measurement dynamics; it is NOT a composition plan, a prescribed KLAAT phase, or evidence that the body is 'entrained' to Ankora.

A stronger model would represent separate candidate event series: introductory speech, visual reveal, first audible drone, first drum attack, major spontaneous acoustic changes, and any voluntary participant movement. But adding regressors does not automatically identify causal effects. If every performance always follows the same introduction with the same delay, the cue and music remain tightly correlated. No mathematical technique can create independent variation that the experiment never contained.

This is a problem of IDENTIFIABILITY. When two potential causes always occur together or at a fixed short interval, their separate contributions cannot be confidently estimated from a single observed response trace. Model fit may be excellent while the causal story remains ambiguous.

7. THE CONTRADICTION: REAL ANTICIPATION IS PART OF THE ART, BUT NOT THE SOUND'S EFFECT

KLAAT has an intentional ancient/ritual/archaeosonic identity. A dark performance space, the sight of an agave instrument, the physical presence of an elk-hide drum, and the expectation of an unusual acoustic event may all be artistically meaningful. The goal is not to strip KLAAT of these things or pretend that the live ritual is identical to an audio-only recording. The artistic event can legitimately include anticipation.

Scientific claims need a more exact object. 'People reacted during the KLAAT event' may include visual presentation, anticipation, room context, social expectation, and sound. 'The Amastacia drone caused this skin-conductance response' is a narrower claim requiring evidence that distinguishes those influences. Neither statement should be quietly substituted for the other.

This also matters for KLAAT as an everyday soundtrack. A person pressing play on an unlabeled recording while cleaning has different anticipatory cues from someone watching Paul prepare the instruments in a ritual setting. The same musical recording may be associated with different experiences. Those differences are not failures of authenticity; they are distinct listening conditions.

8. PHYSIOLOGICAL CHANNELS DO NOT SHARE ONE LATENCY

A future researcher might collect EDA, heart rate, respiration, visible goosebumps, and voluntary reports. These are not five identical sensors of 'trance.' Each has different temporal resolution, biological determinants, and response dynamics. Heart rate is influenced by breathing, posture, movement, and autonomic regulation; respiration can be voluntarily adjusted; piloerection may be intermittent and body-site dependent; subjective chills can occur without visible piloerection. A response observed earlier on one channel does not establish that it caused a later response on another.

For KLAAT, this means that aligning every channel to the nearest Ankora strike would be a category error. Ankora's fast attack sequence, Amastacia's sustained breath and harmonics, the listener's changing attention, and autonomic response windows unfold on different timescales. A three-second EDA rise can encompass many Ankora attacks. Without independent variation and a justified model, the data cannot identify which individual strike 'caused' the rise.

9. A CAUSAL DESIGN THAT DOES NOT GOVERN THE PERFORMANCE

If KLAAT ever develops a physiological pilot, three distinctions can make the interpretation more defensible without prescribing a note, rhythm, tempo, or phase to Paul.

First, keep an uncued baseline where feasible. This means a quiet interval before an evocative introduction or instrument reveal, not merely a pause after the listener has been told what to expect. A quiet baseline is still not psychologically empty, and equipment attachment itself may change arousal. Its value is that the timing is documented.

Second, mark the entire cue timeline. Record when the listener enters, sees the instruments, hears a description, receives a start signal, first hears Amastacia, first hears Ankora, and begins or stops any voluntary movement. Distinguish the acoustic file's zero from the sound reaching the listener. The goal is not to add elaborate production requirements to a small informal pilot; it is to avoid false precision if formal physiology is ever collected.

Third, if a later controlled study genuinely needs to estimate the effect of framing, compare neutral and evocative introductions with the SAME unedited authentic recording, assigned across different listeners or appropriately counterbalanced occasions. Predefine a primary subjective outcome and an analysis of the cue period. Keep listening level and delivery comparable. Avoid showing an individual the same short clip repeatedly and then treating each response as an independent new listener. Even with randomization, such a design estimates an effect of the framing condition in that sample; it does not prove which specific thought or neural mechanism generated it.

A natural live KLAAT performance answers a different question: what happens in the complete artistic situation? Both questions can matter. They should not be collapsed into a single 'resonance efficacy' number.

10. HOW TO INTERPRET FOUR POSSIBLE RESULTS

CASE A — A response begins before audio. Observation: a physiological event is detected in the cue period. Warranted conclusion: the event cannot have been caused by audio that had not yet occurred, although anticipation, carryover, spontaneous fluctuation, and other cues remain competing explanations. It may still be part of the complete live experience.

CASE B — A response begins just after audio. Observation: a sensor change follows sound onset. Warranted conclusion: temporal ordering alone does not establish the sound as cause, because physiological lag can shift a cue-driven response across time zero. Inspect earlier cue timing and model assumptions.

CASE C — A response begins well into an unedited performance. Observation: a delayed physiological change occurs while Amastacia and Ankora continue. Warranted conclusion: the change may be associated with acoustic events, anticipation of what follows, attention, movement, ordinary drift, or other causes. Do not assign it to a specific drum strike merely because one occurs nearby.

CASE D — No detectable physiological change. Observation: no response exceeds a prespecified threshold on the measured channel. Warranted conclusion: the selected channel did not show a detectable event under those conditions. It does not prove that the listener felt nothing, was not absorbed, or did not find the music energizing. The sensor may be insensitive to the experience of interest.

These cases protect against both exaggerated positive claims and equally unwarranted negative claims. A listener's own description remains evidence of their subjective experience; it is not a direct measure of autonomic cause.

11. WHAT WOULD BE STRONG EVIDENCE, AND WHAT WOULD NOT?

ESTABLISHED FROM EXTERNAL PRIMARY RESEARCH: - Skin conductance responses have delayed and overlapping dynamics; raw peaks need not correspond one-to-one to nearby events. - In a controlled paired-stimulus experiment, raw responses merged at short interstimulus intervals while model-based phasic estimates could separate them. - Visible piloerection was sometimes detected before audio began in a laboratory trial containing a description period. - Chills and piloerection are not unique signatures of one emotion or one sensory source.

CONTEXT-DEPENDENT: - The shape and latency of EDA responses vary with individuals, stimulus type, task, scoring algorithm, and sensor quality. - An evocative KLAAT introduction may or may not change a listener's responses. Existing studies do not provide a KLAAT effect size. - A model may separate overlapping responses statistically without uniquely identifying the causal cue.

PLAUSIBLE, TESTABLE KLAAT HYPOTHESES: - Some apparent early 'sound-evoked' autonomic events in a full live KLAAT presentation will reflect pre-audio anticipation or cue-related activity. - Audio-only listening with neutral instructions will yield a different early-response distribution from a fully staged presentation, even for the same unedited take. - Listener descriptions of bodily activation and objectively measured EDA will sometimes disagree, because they index different phenomena.

SPECULATION: - The distinctive visual identity of Amastacia and Ankora could make some listeners especially anticipatory before the first sound. No KLAAT listener dataset currently tests this.

UNSUPPORTED CLAIMS: - KLAAT has already produced physiological trance or neural entrainment. - A post-onset skin-conductance rise proves that Amastacia or Ankora caused it. - A cue-period goosebump proves ritual symbolism activated a specific brain system. - A particular C# frequency has a special autonomic effect. - A deconvolution program can identify the true psychological cause of an individual response from a single trace. - Stronger sensor responses necessarily mean greater pleasure, absorption, safety, or therapeutic benefit.

12. THE PRACTICAL SCIENTIFIC ADVANCE

The new durable rule is the RESPONSE-ORIGIN GATE: before calling a physiological event 'music-evoked,' distinguish the time of the potential cause from the later time at which the body made that event measurable. Preserve the cue record, the audio timeline, the raw sensor trace, the scoring rule, and uncertainty about latency. If a potential cue and the sound are inseparable in the design, report the event as associated with the complete listening situation, not causally attributable to the sound alone.

This does not diminish the power of KLAAT as art. The research project is most useful when it prevents an attractive story from outrunning what was actually observed. Paul's improvisation remains spontaneous; the study changes how future evidence would be interpreted, not how he should play.

13. OPTIONAL, LOW-PRESSURE KLAAT OBSERVATION

No sensor purchase or formal experiment is necessary. The next time an existing KLAAT clip is shared with a willing listener, simply note whether they heard it cold, received a description first, or saw the instruments beforehand. Invite an unprompted reaction after the clip, and preserve their own words. Do not suggest that they should feel goosebumps, trance, energy, or bodily vibration. One informal response cannot isolate the effect of the introduction, but it can prevent a future research log from mistaking different listening contexts for identical ones. Keep the music exactly as played.
