Ankora Feel Stronger by Giving the Pulse Somewhere to Land

SESSION 052 / 164Pulse & RhythmRESEARCH SESSION

Session 51 established a compositional principle that matters deeply to KLAAT: repetition is not empty time. A stable field teaches the listener what to expect, and a later omission or deviation can become perceptually large without adding physical energy.

The strongest KLAAT-relevant finding in the new hourly research extends that principle from temporal expectation into the body.

The question is deceptively simple:

If Amastacia is already producing a sustained bed of felt vibration, does adding more Ankora vibration always make the Pulse feel stronger?

The evidence says we should not assume so.

In tactile perception, a background vibration can make another vibration harder to detect. This is vibrotactile masking. The effect depends on timing, level, frequency, body site, contact conditions, and which tactile pathways are being stimulated. A sufficiently strong or prolonged masker can raise the threshold for a subsequent tactile signal, and forward masking generally weakens as more time separates the masker from the test signal.

For KLAAT, that suggests a new possibility:

ANKORA MAY SOMETIMES BECOME MORE BODILY LEGIBLE WHEN AMASTACIA MOMENTARILY GIVES IT SPACE.

This is not a claim that Amastacia and Ankora are currently masking one another in live performance. We have not measured that. It is a mechanism strong enough to test.

1. MORE VIBRATION IS NOT NECESSARILY MORE PULSE

KLAAT has repeatedly encountered the temptation to treat bodily intensity as a single quantity: more low-frequency energy, more floor movement, more contact vibration, therefore more somatic impact.

That model is too simple.

Imagine a continuous mechanical background produced by Amastacia. Ankora then adds a transient whose energy overlaps the same tactile channel. The nervous system does not necessarily experience the transient as “background plus new signal” with perfect separation. The background can reduce the perceptual contrast of the transient.

The useful analogy is not loudness. It is visibility.

A bright flash is conspicuous in darkness and less conspicuous against another bright source. The flash itself has not changed. The background has changed the contrast.

Tactile perception has analogous masking and increment-detection effects.

So KLAAT gains a new variable:

TACTILE SIGNAL-TO-BACKGROUND RELATIONSHIP.

This is not simply the ratio of two accelerometer numbers. It is a perceptual relationship whose physical contributors include magnitude, frequency overlap, temporal overlap, contact pathway, and sensory adaptation.

2. FORWARD MASKING GIVES TIME A PHYSICAL ROLE

Classic vibrotactile work has shown that a preceding masker can impair detection of a later tactile target and that the effect depends strongly on the interval between masker offset and target onset. Masker amplitude and duration also matter.

That gives KLAAT a remarkable compositional possibility.

A short reduction in Amastacia-derived mechanical background immediately before an Ankora strike might make the strike feel more distinct even if Ankora itself is not played harder.

The important structure would be:

DRONE → MICRO-OPENING → PULSE.

The micro-opening does not have to be silence. It could be a brief reduction in the component of Amastacia that is coupling most strongly into a particular surface or body pathway.

This is what we will call TACTILE NEGATIVE SPACE.

The term is a KLAAT compositional construct, not a standard scientific metric.

Tactile negative space means:

A deliberately created reduction in competing mechanical/tactile background that increases the perceptual contrast available to a subsequent event.

The artistic consequence is substantial.

KLAAT may be able to increase bodily definition by subtracting energy before a Pulse rather than adding energy to the Pulse.

3. FREQUENCY OVERLAP MATTERS

Tactile masking is not completely frequency-blind. Research on vibrotactile masking shows frequency-selective behavior under some conditions: masking tends to be stronger when masker and target engage overlapping tactile processing channels, although the exact pattern depends on frequency range, body site, stimulus geometry, and intensity.

That matters because Amastacia and Ankora do not produce single frequencies.

Amastacia’s nominal C# identity does not mean the body receives only one sinusoid. The instrument produces a harmonic and time-varying spectrum, and the room/structure can selectively reinforce parts of it.

Ankora’s attack is even broader. Its transient contains a changing spectrum whose mechanical transmission depends on drum technique, source coupling, floor construction, listener contact, and position.

Therefore the useful KLAAT question is not:

“Does C# mask the drum?”

It is:

“AT THE LISTENER’S ACTUAL CONTACT PATHWAY, WHICH AMASTACIA-DERIVED VIBRATION BANDS OVERLAP THE MECHANICALLY SALIENT PARTS OF ANKORA’S ATTACK?”

That is a measurement question, not a symbolic-frequency question.

If overlap is substantial, a narrow reduction in Amastacia’s mechanically transmitted energy around that region might improve Pulse definition without requiring a broadband volume dip.

If overlap is weak, the tactic may do little.

4. THE BODY DOES NOT HAVE ONE TACTILE CHANNEL

The hourly research also places a hard boundary around this idea.

Results from fingertip psychophysics cannot simply be transplanted onto a standing or seated KLAAT listener.

Whole-body and body-site vibration sensitivity varies with frequency, posture, direction, contact force, and body location. The feet, pelvis/back, and torso are not interchangeable sensors. Standing can change foot sensitivity relative to sitting. Torso sites differ from one another. Whole-body vertical vibration has its own frequency-dependent perceptual behavior.

Therefore “Amastacia masks Ankora” is too broad to be a useful hypothesis.

The correct KLAAT hypothesis is pathway-specific:

Amastacia may mask some mechanically transmitted features of Ankora through a particular body-contact pathway under particular conditions.

This is why Sessions 49 and 50 mattered.

Audio–tactile coherence told us that timing between channels may matter.

Body–surface coupling told us that the interface changes what reaches the listener.

Session 52 now adds:

BACKGROUND–TARGET CONTRAST MAY DETERMINE HOW CLEARLY THAT COUPLED PULSE IS FELT.

These are not three unrelated technical lessons. They are becoming one KLAAT model.

5. AMASTACIA AS FIELD, ANKORA AS FIGURE

This suggests a particularly useful way to conceptualize the two core instruments.

AMASTACIA creates FIELD.

ANKORA creates FIGURE.

That is not absolute. Amastacia can articulate and Ankora can create sustained resonance. But as a compositional model it is powerful.

A figure becomes legible partly because it differs from its field.

If the field fills every available sensory dimension at maximum density, the figure may have less perceptual room.

KLAAT therefore does not necessarily want Amastacia to maximize bodily vibration continuously.

A more sophisticated Drone may establish a heavy, convincing field while retaining the ability to withdraw selectively.

That withdrawal can become part of the Pulse.

This changes the relationship between the instruments:

Ankora does not merely enter ON TOP OF Amastacia.

Amastacia can actively SHAPE THE SPACE INTO WHICH ANKORA ENTERS.

That is a more integrated composition system.

6. MICRO-DIPS ARE NOT YET A PERFORMANCE RULE

The hourly research proposed brief pre-Pulse reductions on the order of tens to low hundreds of milliseconds as an experimental range.

That is reasonable for testing, but it must not become doctrine.

Laboratory forward-masking timing depends on the exact stimulus and tactile system being studied. We do not yet know the recovery time of KLAAT’s floor-, seat-, or torso-mediated pathways.

A 50 ms dip may be effective.

A 150 ms dip may be effective.

Either could be too short, too long, or musically obvious.

The effect could also arise acoustically rather than tactilely if the listener simply hears Amastacia duck before Ankora.

That confound is important.

If Paul intentionally lowers Amastacia before every strike, the ear receives a dynamic contrast too. A listener might report a stronger Pulse because of auditory contrast, tactile contrast, expectation, or all three.

KLAAT should not pretend these are separated unless the experiment actually separates them.

7. THE FOUR-PHASE ARCHITECTURE

GROUND ANCHOR — BUILD THE FIELD

Ground Anchor establishes Amastacia as a continuous sensory reference. For tactile-negative-space research, the important variable is not maximum vibration but a sufficiently stable background from which later contrast can emerge.

The Drone teaches the body what “continuous” feels like.

THRESHOLD GATE — CREATE THE FIRST OPENING

Threshold Gate can be produced not only by Ankora entering but by Amastacia making room for Ankora.

A brief withdrawal immediately before the first Pulse may create a double transition:

FIELD WEAKENS → PULSE ARRIVES.

The Gate is therefore carved before it is struck.

RISING PULSE — SCULPT CONTRAST, NOT JUST DENSITY

This is where the concept becomes most KLAAT-specific.

Rising Pulse does not have to climb by hitting harder and harder. It can increase definition by manipulating the relationship between continuous and transient energy.

Possible strategies include:

continuous Amastacia + Ankora;

small pre-Pulse Amastacia reductions;

selective spectral thinning around some strikes;

alternating high-overlap and low-overlap passages;

periods where Ankora is physically strong but perceptually embedded;

periods where the same Ankora level emerges from newly created negative space.

This gives Rising Pulse another axis besides tempo, density, and loudness:

CONTRAST.

RETURN PATH — LET THE FIELD RECLAIM THE FIGURE

Return Path can reverse the process.

Instead of simply slowing Ankora, Amastacia can gradually stop yielding space around it. Pulse definition may soften before Ankora itself disappears.

Then Ankora is removed completely, leaving only the field.

That creates a potentially elegant perceptual trajectory:

FIELD → FIGURE EMERGES → FIGURE SHARPENS → FIGURE REABSORBS → FIELD.

That architecture is recognizably KLAAT.

8. ACTIVATION WITHOUT ESCALATION

This session reinforces one of the most important developing principles in the entire research program:

KLAAT SHOULD NOT REQUIRE CONTINUOUS ENERGY ESCALATION TO CREATE CONTINUOUS PERCEPTUAL DEVELOPMENT.

If tactile negative space works, Rising Pulse can become more bodily articulated while:

Ankora strike level remains stable,

overall acoustic exposure remains similar or decreases,

and total mechanical energy may even fall briefly before important strikes.

That is a stronger compositional system than “make it louder.”

It also aligns with Session 51’s missing-beat logic.

Both mechanisms exploit subtraction.
