SESSION 16 — MISSING BEAT: PREDICTION ERROR WITHOUT SOUND
ARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT VERBATIM


Core lesson
Once a steady pulse establishes a reliable temporal rule, the nervous system begins predicting when the next event should occur. If an expected strike is omitted, the absence itself becomes perceptually meaningful. A missing event can therefore carry information even though no new acoustic energy was added.


In KLAAT this means silence can function as a temporal event. A single omission after sufficient repetition can create tension, surprise, and bodily anticipation precisely because the listener had prepared for something that never arrived.


Evidence boundary
Established: auditory systems generate predictions about regular timing; omission responses and mismatch-related neural activity are well documented under many conditions. Plausible: a missing Ankora strike may feel bodily because an expected auditory-tactile event fails to arrive. Unsupported: interpreting that omission response as a specific therapeutic reset.


KLAAT test
Maintain a simple Ankora pattern for roughly four minutes. Omit three single strikes at widely separated moments. Ask listeners which omission felt strongest and whether they noticed the absence before they consciously identified what had changed.
