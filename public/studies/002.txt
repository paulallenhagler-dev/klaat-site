SESSION 2 — HOW THE BODY ACTUALLY FEELS VIBRATION
ARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT VERBATIM
Core lesson
The body senses vibration through mechanoreception as well as hearing. Different receptor systems respond to different kinds of skin deformation and vibration. Pacinian and Meissner corpuscles are important examples, but tactile perception depends on frequency, contact area, body site, amplitude, posture, and coupling.


KLAAT must distinguish airborne vibration from mechanical coupling. Ankora can reach a listener through air, but it can also excite a floor, chair, or platform, which then transmits motion into the body. Auditory and tactile events can be integrated into one experience even though they arrive through different sensory pathways.


Evidence boundary
Established: vibrotactile perception is real and frequency-dependent; different mechanoreceptor channels contribute differently. Context-dependent: therapeutic claims about vibroacoustic interventions vary by protocol and evidence quality.


KLAAT test
Compare Ankora at the same playing intensity under two conditions: listener on a rigidly coupled surface versus a more isolated/padded condition. Rate what is heard separately from what is physically felt.
