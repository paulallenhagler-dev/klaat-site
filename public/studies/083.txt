THE SOUND THAT CONTINUES WHEN THE SIGNAL DOES NOT
Auditory Restoration, Masked Breath Transitions, and the Perceptual Integrity of Amastacia
Published: October 13, 2026 — 8:18 AM CDT
Guiding principle: EXPERIENCE-LED CREATION. SCIENCE-INFORMED REFINEMENT.
Evidence status: No KLAAT-specific demonstration of auditory restoration, trance, neural entrainment, or physiological effects.


EDITORIAL ABSTRACT


Session 82 separated Paul's actual breathing, the breath-related features audible in Amastacia, and the listener's own respiration. That distinction reveals a deeper perceptual question. A drone can seem continuous for at least three different reasons: it really is acoustically continuous; a small physical interruption is inaudible because other sounds mask it; or the auditory system constructs the experience of a continuing sound across a masked interruption. Laboratory research has demonstrated this third phenomenon, usually called the auditory continuity illusion or auditory induction, with carefully controlled interrupted tones and masking noises. It is not established that Ankora's brief attacks induce the same effect on Amastacia, nor that a smooth circular-breathing transition involves any physical gap at all. The opportunity is to learn how the listener hears a living, continuous world despite fluctuations in the performance—without telling Paul to hide his breath, quantize his drumming, or organize the music around a laboratory model. This session separates real continuity, masking, perceptual restoration, and attention; explains the evidence and its limitations; and ends with one optional retrospective listening comparison using existing KLAAT audio.


1. THE UNRESOLVED QUESTION AFTER SESSION 82


Circular breathing allows Amastacia to maintain an outward-driven tone while Paul briefly inhales through his nose. Session 82 established that the physical switch of air sources, an audible breath-like change, and the listener's respiratory response are not the same event. But another gap remains: even if an acoustic analyzer finds a brief dip in Amastacia's signal, a listener may not hear the drone as broken. Conversely, a conspicuous change of harmonic emphasis can feel like a musical interruption even when the sound never falls silent.


This is a question about auditory objecthood: what makes an evolving sound seem to belong to one continuing source? It matters to KLAAT's character as much as its measurable frequency content. Amastacia is not simply a fixed C# tone. She is a breath-driven, harmonically active source with roughness, pressure fluctuations, noise and articulations. Ankora places discrete, resonant attacks into the same acoustic scene. The result can feel like one sustained environment containing motion. We should understand how that unity is heard before making claims about why it is absorbing.


2. THREE DIFFERENT WAYS A DRONE CAN SEEM UNBROKEN


REAL ACOUSTIC CONTINUITY: The relevant components of Amastacia remain present throughout a transition, perhaps at changing amplitude or timbre. A spectrogram may show a harmonic series continuing through a small envelope dip. There is no missing sound for the auditory system to reconstruct. This is the straightforward explanation and should be considered first.


MASKED INTERRUPTION: Amastacia physically weakens or stops briefly, but another event—potentially a sufficiently strong, spectrally overlapping Ankora strike—makes that loss difficult to detect. Masking can prevent the listener from detecting a gap. This does not automatically mean the listener perceptually restores the missing tone; 'I did not notice a gap' is weaker evidence than 'I heard the drone continue during the gap.'


PERCEPTUAL RESTORATION: A physically interrupted sound is heard as if it continued behind a plausible masking sound. Laboratory demonstrations often replace a segment of a tone with appropriately intense noise. The auditory system represents an apparently persistent source even though part of the target signal is absent. This is a constructive perceptual effect, not an actual restoration of acoustic energy in the room.


These categories must not be collapsed. A listener's report that KLAAT 'never stops' could arise from any of them, from attention to the larger performance, or from an ordinary metaphor for musical continuity.


3. THE CLASSIC EXPERIMENTAL EFFECT


In the auditory continuity illusion, a tone interrupted by silence is generally heard as discontinuous. When the silent interruption is occupied by a sufficiently effective noise masker, listeners may instead hear the tone as continuing behind the noise. The masking sound makes it plausible that the ongoing target has been temporarily obscured rather than terminated. The effect depends on properties of the target, interruption, masker and context. It is not a universal rule that 'noise fills gaps.'


Riecke, van Opstal and Formisano (2008) systematically varied interrupting noise duration, relative power and spectral notch width for amplitude-modulated tones at different frequencies. Their psychometric results constrained when listeners reported continuity and supported an auditory-filter account of masking conditions. The crucial lesson is that continuity depends on whether the masker plausibly obscures the target in the relevant spectral region. A drum attack with very little energy near the target's audible components may not support restoration, even if the attack sounds subjectively loud. [S1]


4. A NEURAL CORRELATE IS NOT A KLAAT BRAINWAVE CLAIM


Human fMRI work by Riecke and colleagues (2007) reported auditory cortical activity related to perceived continuity of interrupted tones in noise rather than only the raw physical presence or absence of a tone. That finding indicates that perceptual organization can influence activity in auditory cortex. [S2]


A separate study in awake macaques by Petkov, O'Connor and Sutter (2007) found that some primary auditory cortical neurons responded to interrupted tonal targets under masking noise in a way resembling their responses to uninterrupted targets. This provides important mechanistic evidence, but the species, stimuli and recording methods differ from a human listening to didgeridoo and frame drum. [S3]


In human EEG experiments, Riecke and colleagues (2009) observed changes in slow, approximately 4-Hz stimulus-evoked cortical synchronization when masked gaps were perceptually restored. A later EEG experiment using 3-Hz amplitude-modulated tones likewise associated restoration with changes in low-frequency power and phase locking. These are measurements of neural responses to specifically designed auditory stimuli. They do NOT mean that Amastacia drives the brain to 3 or 4 Hz, that the acoustic drone contains a therapeutic theta frequency, or that the listener has entered trance. [S4, S5]


This distinction is especially important for KLAAT, whose artistic language involves resonance and altered awareness. A laboratory correlate of an illusion is not a general explanation of consciousness.


5. CONTEXT AND EXPECTATION MATTER


Riecke, Mendelsohn, Schreiner and Formisano (2009) showed that the continuity illusion can adapt to the preceding auditory scene. The history of perceived continuity and the loudness of intervening noise influenced later restoration judgments. This suggests that the auditory system does not process each interruption in isolation; it interprets interruptions against a developing acoustic context. [S6]


For KLAAT, the potential relevance is obvious but unproven. Amastacia's sustained sound may establish an expectation of persistence. Ankora's recurring attacks may become familiar events within that sound world. The same brief change in Amastacia might be heard differently early in a performance than after many minutes of continuous drone. That is a reasonable hypothesis, not an observed KLAAT result.


It is equally possible that an especially rough breath transition attracts attention and makes the drone feel less continuous. An event can be aesthetically powerful precisely because it interrupts expectation. Nothing in the restoration literature says a perfectly seamless drone is artistically superior.


6. WHY ANKORA IS NOT A LABORATORY NOISE BURST


The distinction between a broadband masking noise and a frame-drum attack is not trivial. Ankora's sound has a sharp onset, evolving spectral content, resonant decay and performance-dependent dynamics. A brief strike might mask a momentary dip in Amastacia, but its capacity to do so depends on temporal overlap, level, frequency content, recording conditions and listener hearing. The drum might instead make the transition more obvious by drawing attention to the exact instant when the drone changes.


Some masking effects persist briefly after the masker; others depend on simultaneous energy. Forward masking, simultaneous masking, perceptual restoration and attentional distraction are not synonyms. A drum stroke at the same time as an inhale does not automatically produce an auditory continuity illusion.


Ankora may also preserve the musical world's continuity without hiding any acoustic gap at all. The listener could remain oriented to an ongoing rhythm while Amastacia changes or pauses. That is continuity at the level of the performance, not necessarily restoration of a missing tonal segment. This distinction matters because KLAAT contains more than one sound source and more than one time scale.


7. THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE FILE AND THE EXPERIENCE


If we have the original, separate microphone tracks for Amastacia and Ankora, we can inspect whether a drone component actually disappears during a transition. If we have only a mixed stereo recording, a drum transient can obscure Amastacia's energy in both the ears and the analyzer. A dip or apparent disappearance in a spectrogram of the mix cannot confidently identify the physical behavior of the didgeridoo source. Recording compression, automatic gain control, clipping, microphone placement and room reflections can further confuse the picture.


The original file should remain untouched. Any diagnostic gain changes, filtering or muting must be saved as clearly labeled derivative copies. The scientific question is not whether a processed clip sounds better; it is whether a listener's continuity judgment can be explained by what was actually delivered. A spectrogram measures acoustic structure in a recording. It does not measure a subjective impression of an unbroken world.


8. WHY THIS MATTERS TO THE LIVE EXPERIENCE


A live KLAAT performance may offer information that recordings lack: visible movement, the spatial separation of instruments, direct sound and reverberation, and in some conditions physically appreciable low-frequency energy. Visual access to Paul's breathing could reveal a nasal inhale that an audio-only listener misses. Conversely, hearing a deep continuous tone in a reverberant room may make a subtle transition harder to detect.


These are possible confounds, not proven KLAAT effects. A comparison of audio-only and audiovisual listening would need to hold the acoustic stimulus constant. A comparison of live and recorded listening would need to account for room, level, sight and bodily contact. Session 80's delivery-chain discipline therefore remains relevant: the listener hears a particular version of KLAAT under particular conditions.


9. THE ROLE OF ATTENTION AND ABSORPTION


Auditory restoration can help explain why a listener hears a coherent source despite masking, but it does not by itself explain why that listener becomes absorbed. Absorption also involves attention, motivation, context, emotional interpretation, and the larger temporal pattern. A person may hear a continuous drone and feel bored. Another may hear the same drone as intensely alive. A third may find Ankora's interruptions energizing rather than disruptive.


For an everyday soundtrack, the ability to perceive an ongoing musical environment while attending intermittently to work or movement may be valuable. For concentrated listening, small changes of breath and drum attack may become the very features that sustain attention. The two use cases need not produce identical listening behavior. Neither is a lesser form of KLAAT.


10. AMASTACIA AS A PERSISTENT AUDITORY OBJECT


A didgeridoo drone has multiple cues to source identity: harmonic relations, characteristic spectral envelope, spatial location, attack and articulation patterns, and a continuing low-frequency component when faithfully reproduced. These cues may support the impression that Amastacia remains the same instrument even as its sound changes dramatically.


A useful conceptual distinction is between source persistence and signal persistence. A source can remain perceptually present while its signal is temporarily masked. A signal can remain physically continuous while its timbre changes enough that a listener hears a new event. KLAAT's ancient, weathered, archaeosonic aesthetic may be partly connected to the sense of one source enduring through changing surfaces—but that interpretation is artistic speculation, not a laboratory result.


11. THE FOUR PHASES AS OPTIONAL LISTENING DESCRIPTIONS


The established vocabulary of Ground Anchor, Threshold Gate, Rising Pulse and Return Path can still help describe particular naturally occurring KLAAT performances. It is not a required score.


During a passage later described as Ground Anchor, Amastacia may establish source identity and an expectation of persistence. During a naturally occurring Threshold Gate, Ankora may enter the same scene without replacing Amastacia as the perceived continuous source. During a passage described as Rising Pulse, increased drum activity might compete with or protect the audibility of subtle drone changes. During a Return Path, the thinning of Ankora may reveal Amastacia's breath details again. These are candidate retrospective descriptions, not instructions to perform four sections or make particular changes.


If an authentic performance never presents these transitions, that is not a failure. The framework should describe the music when useful and step aside when it is not.


12. ESTABLISHED EVIDENCE


Established within controlled experimental conditions: listeners can perceive interrupted tonal sounds as continuing when an intervening masker plausibly obscures the missing sound. Continuity judgments depend on the spectral and temporal relationship of masker and target. Human neuroimaging and EEG, and macaque single-unit recordings, have identified neural correlates of illusory continuity. Prior auditory context can influence subsequent restoration judgments. None of these findings requires a mystical mechanism. [S1–S6]


Established as a methodological distinction: an actual uninterrupted sound, a physically interrupted but undetected sound, and a physically interrupted sound perceptually restored as continuing are not the same claim. Listener report, waveform analysis and neural recording address different levels of explanation.


13. CONTEXT-DEPENDENT EVIDENCE


The classic stimuli are controlled tones and noise, not naturally improvised didgeridoo with frame drum. Effective masking depends on spectral overlap, relative level, interruption duration and listening context. The neural studies use particular tasks and signal types; they do not establish universal cortical signatures for natural music. A room, microphone, headphone or phone speaker can change the balance between Amastacia and Ankora. Listening instructions can influence whether a person notices a local gap or attends to the larger musical flow.


A particularly important counterpoint is that continuity illusions are not guaranteed. Silent interruptions often sound like interruptions. Noise with insufficient masking power, or with a spectral notch that exposes the missing target, can fail to produce restoration. Strongly differentiated drum attacks may highlight rather than conceal an Amastacia transition. The correct outcome for KLAAT is an empirical question.


14. PLAUSIBLE AND TESTABLE KLAAT HYPOTHESES


H1 — In some authentic recordings, listeners will report Amastacia as continuous despite a small measurable reduction in its sound during a breath transition.


H2 — An Ankora attack overlapping a transition may reduce detection of a brief Amastacia dip, but this may reflect ordinary masking or attention rather than true perceptual restoration.


H3 — If a genuine, masked Amastacia gap exists, listeners may sometimes report the drone as persisting through it; this would be the closest KLAAT-specific analogue to the laboratory continuity illusion.


H4 — Long exposure to Amastacia's persistent timbre may influence whether a subsequent ambiguous interruption is interpreted as a continuing source.


H5 — Some listeners may find audible breath irregularities more engaging than an acoustically smooth transition. Continuity is not a universal measure of quality.


Each hypothesis requires appropriate recorded material and listener reports. None is established by Paul's own experience of flow.


15. SPECULATION


The impression of a single ancient sound world surviving breath changes, drum strikes and texture shifts may arise partly from auditory object persistence. A listener might experience Ankora not as a competing instrument but as activity occurring within a continuous Amastacia environment. That could contribute to absorption or the usefulness of KLAAT as an energizing daily-life soundtrack. This is a possible artistic-perceptual interpretation, not a demonstrated mechanism of trance or autonomic activation.


16. UNSUPPORTED CLAIMS


It would be unsupported to claim that Ankora automatically hides circular-breathing gaps; that any undetected gap is proof of neural restoration; that Amastacia's C# pitch has a unique continuity or healing property; that a 3–4 Hz EEG correlate of a laboratory illusion demonstrates theta entrainment in KLAAT; that smoother breath transitions necessarily create deeper trance; that listeners receive Paul's breathing physiology through the sound; or that a waveform proves what listeners consciously heard.


The available KLAAT record does not yet contain controlled listener judgments of physically verified drone interruptions. Claims about trance, neural oscillations, physiological coupling or healing remain untested for KLAAT.


17. METHODOLOGY UPDATE — THE CONTINUITY-CLAIM LADDER


This session adds a practical evidence ladder, not a new compositional rule.


LEVEL 1 — CONTINUOUS SOURCE: The original Amastacia recording shows no relevant physical gap. Listener continuity is expected; do not call it restoration.


LEVEL 2 — AMBIGUOUS SIGNAL: The mixed recording suggests a dip, but the source track or recording conditions cannot establish whether the drone stopped. Record uncertainty; do not classify the mechanism.


LEVEL 3 — VERIFIED INTERRUPTION, LOW DETECTABILITY: A source gap is documented, but listeners fail to notice it. Masking and attention remain viable explanations.


LEVEL 4 — VERIFIED INTERRUPTION, PERCEIVED PERSISTENCE: A documented gap coincides with plausible masking, and listeners specifically report hearing the drone persist through the interval more often than in an appropriate comparison. This supports an auditory-restoration interpretation, with limits.


LEVEL 5 — REPLICATED MECHANISM: Independent recordings, controlled masker manipulations, reliable listener judgments and replication distinguish restoration from simple gap nondetection. Only then consider a durable KLAAT-specific perceptual claim. No neurological claim follows automatically.


Keep performer mechanics, acoustic source, mixed recording, listener report and optional physiology on separate records. The KSV-0.1 vocabulary remains provisional and descriptive, not a score. There is no reason to alter Paul's spontaneous playing to satisfy this ladder.


18. TECHNICAL FLUENCY WITHOUT ERASING HUMAN GRAIN


Circular-breathing practice can improve the reliability of an intended continuous drone. But a technically smooth switch is not the same thing as an artistically compelling performance. Amastacia's roughness, breath noise, momentary pressure changes and harmonic gestures may be integral to the identity of KLAAT. Ankora's naturally varied attacks may give those gestures contrast and movement.


The best research use is diagnostic: when Paul wants continuity, what causes an unwanted break? When a breath transition sounds alive rather than disruptive, what does the recording actually contain? When a listener calls a performance 'seamless,' do they mean literally uninterrupted tone, uninterrupted rhythmic orientation, or an unbroken feeling of immersion? These questions support skill and understanding without demanding a change of aesthetic.


19. QUANTITATIVE DISCIPLINE AND LIMITATIONS


The cited psychoacoustic studies manipulated variables such as masker power, gap duration and spectral notch width, but their threshold values cannot be imported as universal KLAAT settings. A narrowband harmonic drone, resonant drum and reverberant room differ from laboratory stimuli. It would be misleading to invent a maximum 'hidden inhale duration' or a recommended drum loudness from those results.


If a pilot is conducted, report the number of distinct transitions, listener judgments per transition, and which source-track conditions were actually verified. Multiple ratings from the same person or the same recording are not independent replications. Avoid claiming a physiological mechanism from a handful of subjective judgments. If an observation is inconclusive, that is a useful outcome because it identifies the next measurement requirement.


20. THE MOST IMPORTANT CONTRADICTION


A perceptually unbroken drone is not necessarily a more absorbing drone. A detectable breath gesture might strengthen the sense of human presence; a hidden transition might preserve an uninterrupted Field; an unexpected break might create emotional tension. All three can be valid KLAAT experiences. Auditory continuity research explains one aspect of how a sound can remain perceptually present. It does not rank those experiences aesthetically.


This contradiction is productive. It keeps science in its proper role: understanding how the listener hears what Paul already makes, not deciding which moments he should play.
