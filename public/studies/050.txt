KLAAT RESONANCE RESEARCH — SESSION 50
THE BODY–SURFACE INTERFACE: WHY THE FLOOR IS NOT THE STIMULUS


Session 49 established audio–tactile coherence as a serious KLAAT variable: an Ankora strike or Amastacia modulation may be more bodily salient when airborne and mechanically transmitted information agree in time. The strongest unresolved finding from the hourly research now sits one step earlier in the chain.


Before vibration reaches a listener, it must cross an interface.


That interface may be a bare foot on wood, a shoe sole on concrete, the pelvis on a chair, a back against a seat, a hand on a platform, or several of those at once. The surface can be vibrating measurably, yet that does not tell us how much of that motion actually enters the body or how it is distributed across mechanoreceptive systems.


For KLAAT, this creates a crucial correction:


PLATFORM VIBRATION ≠ VIBRATION DELIVERED TO THE BODY ≠ VIBRATION PERCEIVED BY THE LISTENER.


The missing variable is BODY–SURFACE COUPLING.


WHY THIS MATTERS TO KLAAT


KLAAT deliberately works with physical sound. Amastacia produces a sustained low-frequency-rich acoustic field. Ankora contributes sharper transients and repeated pulse structure. In a responsive room, some of that energy becomes structure-borne motion.


It is tempting to measure the floor, see a larger acceleration value, and conclude that the listener is receiving “more vibration.”


That inference is too strong.


Mechanical transmission depends on how the vibrating structure is loaded and how the body contacts it. A listener standing in thick shoes, standing barefoot, sitting with both feet planted, sitting with feet lifted, or leaning firmly into a chair back can all occupy the same point in the room while presenting different mechanical boundary conditions.


The venue therefore does not end at the floor.


For KLAAT, the complete mechanical pathway is better represented as:


INSTRUMENT → AIR / STRUCTURE → FLOOR OR SEAT → CONTACT INTERFACE → BODY → PERCEPTION.


Every arrow can alter the signal.


CONTACT AREA CHANGES WHAT “THE SAME VIBRATION” MEANS


Vibrotactile psychophysics has repeatedly shown that perception depends on more than frequency and amplitude. Contact area, contact force, body site and stimulus geometry can change thresholds and perceived intensity.


This is not mysterious. Different skin regions have different receptor densities and mechanical properties, and a larger contact area can recruit more receptors or allow spatial summation under some conditions. Increasing contact force can also change the mechanical coupling between a vibrating probe and tissue.


Research by Morioka and Griffin, and later Forta, Griffin and Morioka, demonstrated that vibrotactile thresholds vary with body location and contact conditions. At 30 Hz on hairy torso skin, increasing contactor force lowered thresholds in the tested conditions, and sensitivity differed across torso locations. Those controlled probe studies are not a direct model of standing on a KLAAT floor, but they establish the important principle: the physical interface is part of the tactile stimulus.


This immediately invalidates a future shortcut:


“P3 floor acceleration was X, therefore every listener at P3 received X.”


No. The accelerometer received X.


The listener received whatever the floor–body system transmitted.


THE FLOOR CAN BE BOTH RESONATOR AND FILTER


Earlier KLAAT sessions established that the room is not acoustically neutral. The same is true mechanically.


A suspended wooden floor can flex. A concrete slab behaves differently. A stage platform can have structural modes. A chair can introduce its own resonances and damping. Soft upholstery can attenuate some components while transmitting others.


That means a surface can transform Amastacia and Ankora in at least four ways:


MAGNITUDE — how much motion reaches the contact point.


SPECTRUM — which vibration frequencies are enhanced or attenuated.


TIMING — whether Ankora’s attack remains sharp or becomes mechanically smeared.


SPATIAL DISTRIBUTION — where on the structure the motion is strongest.


Session 49 gave us temporal fidelity. Session 50 adds the physical reason temporal fidelity may change before the body ever processes it.


A floor that “vibrates more” is not automatically a better KLAAT floor.


It may have a strong narrow structural resonance that produces a large RMS acceleration while blurring Ankora’s transient pattern. Another floor might show less total acceleration but preserve the pulse envelope more faithfully.


For KLAAT, useful coupling may therefore mean the right transformation, not maximum motion.


AMASTACIA AND ANKORA SHOULD NOT BE EXPECTED TO COUPLE IDENTICALLY


Amastacia and Ankora excite the room differently.


Amastacia is a sustained air-column source. Its mechanical contribution may emerge through airborne excitation of surfaces, direct performer/instrument contact, low-frequency room pressure, or combinations of those pathways.


Ankora produces transient force and a broad time-varying spectrum. Depending on how it is held and how Paul contacts the floor or stage while playing, some mechanical energy may also reach the structure through the performer.


This creates an unresolved KLAAT question:


When a listener feels the Pulse, how much is caused by airborne sound driving the body directly, airborne sound driving the structure, and structure-borne energy entering through contact?


We do not yet know.


And we should not pretend one accelerometer can answer it.


POSTURE IS A KLAAT VARIABLE


Posture changes contact.


Standing emphasizes feet and legs as the obvious structural interface.


Sitting can add pelvis, thighs, back and feet.


Leaning can increase contact force at the torso.


Footwear inserts another mechanical layer.


The hourly research also found that tactile sensitivity itself varies substantially across body sites. Therefore posture changes both the physical transfer path and the sensory tissue receiving that transfer.


That makes posture more than audience comfort.


It can become part of the KLAAT staging system.


A standing KLAAT ritual and a seated KLAAT ritual may not be mechanically equivalent even when the acoustic field is identical.


This does NOT mean one posture is inherently better.


It means we now have a testable design variable.


THE FOUR-PHASE ARCHITECTURE


GROUND ANCHOR


Ground Anchor can establish the baseline body–surface relationship. If Amastacia creates sustained mechanical activity, this phase lets us observe whether the listener gradually becomes aware of the surface without Ankora providing obvious transient cues.


THRESHOLD GATE


Threshold Gate can change coupling without necessarily changing acoustic level. A posture shift, contact change, or movement onto a more responsive surface could create a perceptual threshold event even if Amastacia remains acoustically stable.


This is artistically interesting because it creates another path to transition that does not depend on becoming louder.


RISING PULSE


Rising Pulse is where coupling becomes most consequential. Ankora’s repeated attacks provide a temporally structured mechanical signal. A good interface may preserve those attacks as distinct bodily events. A poor or highly resonant interface may turn them into a less articulated wash.


The question becomes not “How hard is Ankora hitting the floor?” but “How much of the Pulse survives the entire structure–contact–body chain?”


RETURN PATH


Return Path may exploit decoupling as subtraction. Removing a contact pathway, changing posture, stopping Ankora, or moving away from a responsive surface could reduce bodily salience before the airborne Drone disappears.


That gives Return Path another compositional resource: the body can lose the Pulse before the ear loses the field.


EVIDENCE BOUNDARY


ESTABLISHED


Vibrotactile sensitivity depends on frequency, body site, contact area, contact force and other interface conditions. Controlled studies show substantial variation across body locations and contact configurations.


ESTABLISHED


A structural acceleration measurement describes motion at the sensor location. It does not by itself quantify the mechanical stimulus delivered throughout a listener’s body or predict subjective intensity universally.


ESTABLISHED


Posture and contact geometry alter the mechanical boundary conditions between a vibrating structure and a human body.


CONTEXT-DEPENDENT


Probe-based tactile thresholds and laboratory whole-body-vibration results cannot be numerically transplanted into live KLAAT. Floors, seats, footwear, body loading, source spectra and exposure durations differ too much.


PLAUSIBLE / TESTABLE


Two listeners exposed to similar floor acceleration can report different bodily salience partly because their body–surface coupling differs.


PLAUSIBLE / TESTABLE


A mechanically responsive floor or seat can increase KLAAT bodily salience without a corresponding increase in airborne LAeq.


PLAUSIBLE / TESTABLE


Temporal fidelity of Ankora’s structure-borne Pulse may matter more than maximum broadband acceleration for pulse clarity.


SPECULATION


A particular posture or floor construction will prove universally optimal for KLAAT.


UNSUPPORTED


A single acceleration RMS value is a universal “tactile dose.”


UNSUPPORTED


A floor resonance matching Amastacia’s nominal C# is automatically beneficial, therapeutic, or evidence of bodily resonance.


UNSUPPORTED


More structure-borne vibration necessarily means greater activation.


KLAAT METHODOLOGY UPDATE


From this session forward, serious mechanical KLAAT measurements should include a minimum BODY–SURFACE COUPLING record:


measurement location;
acceleration metric and spectrum where valid;
surface type;
listener posture;
body contact sites;
footwear or other interface layer;
approximate contact/loading condition;
duration;
and whether the sensor measures the bare structure or the actual listener contact interface.


This adds a new methodological rule:


MEASURE THE INTERFACE, NOT JUST THE ROOM.


And it sharpens the emerging KLAAT model:


PHYSICAL INPUT → STRUCTURAL TRANSFER → BODY–SURFACE COUPLING → SENSORY HISTORY / ATTENTION / PREDICTION → FELT EXPERIENCE.


The hourly research after Session 49 also identified attention, temporal prediction, learning/order effects and tactile adaptation as important modifiers. Those remain active research directions, but they should not be allowed to obscure the first mechanical question: what physically reached the body?


PRACTICAL EXPERIMENT — KRX-050: THE COUPLING MATRIX


Use one repeatable KLAAT-derived passage containing both sustained Amastacia and clearly articulated Ankora pulse. Keep source position, performance level, room position and passage identical.


At one listener location, compare four standardized contact conditions:


A — standing in normal footwear;
B — standing barefoot or in a thin standardized interface where safe and practical;
C — seated with both feet firmly on the floor;
D — seated with the feet mechanically decoupled from the floor as much as practical.


If equipment allows, record tri-axial acceleration at the floor/contact surface continuously and add a second sensor at the seat or another actual body-contact interface. Record airborne audio simultaneously.


After each randomized condition, collect separate ratings for:


bodily salience;
pulse clarity;
felt location;
activation;
perceived loudness;
comfort/discomfort.


Do not tell the listener which condition is expected to transmit more vibration.


The critical result is not which posture “wins.”


The critical question is whether subjective bodily salience and pulse clarity change substantially while the underlying floor acceleration and airborne acoustic exposure remain similar.


If they do, BODY–SURFACE COUPLING becomes an experimentally demonstrated KLAAT variable rather than merely a methodological caution.


If they do not, its importance for that interface and stimulus is reduced.


OPEN QUESTIONS


Which contact site dominates live KLAAT bodily salience: feet, pelvis, back, or distributed contact? Does footwear meaningfully attenuate the dominant KLAAT vibration bands? Do responsive wooden floors preserve Ankora timing better or worse than rigid slabs? Can seating improve Amastacia bodily salience without making Ankora muddy? How much listener-to-listener variability remains after posture and contact are standardized? And how much of Rising Pulse activation comes from physical coupling versus attention and temporal prediction?


RECOMMENDED NEXT DIRECTION


The strongest next unresolved direction is no longer “more vibration.” It is the interaction between PHYSICAL COUPLING and PERCEPTUAL GAIN.


The hourly research now contains two especially important competing mechanisms: tactile adaptation can make an unchanged stimulus feel weaker over time, while attention, learning and temporal prediction can make unchanged or repeated structure easier to detect or more salient. That opposition is directly relevant to Ground Anchor → Threshold Gate → Rising Pulse → Return Path.


Before KLAAT interprets increasing or decreasing bodily intensity as a change in physical energy, we need to understand how a stable physical stimulus can change perceptually across time.
