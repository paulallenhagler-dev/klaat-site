SESSION 4 — BEATS, PULSATION & AMPLITUDE MODULATION
ARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT VERBATIM
Core lesson
When two nearby frequencies interact, the listener may hear periodic beating at their difference rate. For example, 70 Hz and 74 Hz can create a 4-Hz amplitude fluctuation even though no new 4-Hz acoustic tone has necessarily been generated in the air. Amplitude modulation and beating are temporal patterns imposed on higher-frequency sound.


This matters because KLAAT can create nested periodicities: the main Ankora pulse, slower groupings of that pulse, breath cycles, and pulsation inside the Amastacia spectrum.


Evidence boundary
Established: listeners and auditory pathways respond to amplitude modulation; steady-state responses can track periodic stimulation. Overstated: “4-Hz sound forces the whole brain into theta.” Binaural-beat and brainwave-entrainment claims require careful evidence review.


KLAAT test
Compare a steady Amastacia drone with a deliberately slowly pulsating version, then add Ankora at a different slower organizational level. Listen for whether the result feels like one rhythm, two rhythms, or a hierarchy.
