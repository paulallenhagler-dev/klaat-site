THE DRONE THAT THE MICROPHONE THOUGHT WAS NOISE
Speech-oriented enhancement as an invisible editor of an improvised acoustic performance


GUIDING PRINCIPLE: Experience-led creation. Science-informed refinement.


THE QUESTION


When an unedited KLAAT performance is captured through a phone, video-call app, or social-media recording path, can an algorithm designed to make speech clearer change the apparent relationship between Amastacia's continuous drone and Ankora's attacks? Yes, in principle. Whether it has happened to a particular KLAAT recording cannot be determined without knowing its recording path or making a controlled comparison. The problem is not merely whether a recording sounds 'good.' It is whether the recording still represents the acoustic interaction that actually occurred.


WHY THIS IS A DISTINCT RESEARCH QUESTION


Previous KLAAT studies have examined digital peak overshoot (156), harmonic/percussive separation (157), spectral averaging (155), stereo-to-mono cancellation (160), and listener-dependent attention (159). This session concerns a different point in the signal chain: speech-oriented suppression applied during capture or communication. It can alter the audio before any later analysis, export, or playback. A November 9 hourly research note at 16:19 CST identified this as a provisional unresolved direction. Novelty is bounded: the available notes and recent session summaries were checked, but no claim of an exhaustive search of all historic studies is made.


THE ORIGINAL EVENT AND ITS DIGITAL REPRESENTATION


At the instrument level, Amastacia is not a static pure tone. Its agave body, player's breath, lips and vocal tract generate a changing harmonic spectrum with airflow noise, resonant emphasis, and brief articulations. Ankora is not simply an isolated click: the elk-hide head and drum body continue to resonate after each attack. Together they create a mixed signal containing sustained and transient material, sometimes overlapping in frequency and time.


A speech-optimized processor has a different objective from the artist. It may try to preserve speech while reducing sounds estimated to be background noise. Depending on the implementation, a sustained drone could be classified as undesirable stationary energy; certain percussion transients might be preserved, suppressed, or transformed. These are possible outcomes, not universal properties of all phones or apps. A voice-isolation setting might be off; an app may have music mode; an algorithm may preserve music surprisingly well. The specific implementation matters.


A simple diagnostic model is Y(t,f)=G(t,f)X(t,f), where X is the input short-time spectrum, G is a time- and frequency-dependent gain, and Y is the processed output. If a hypothetical algorithm assigns G=0.25 to part of Amastacia's drone, that component loses 20 log10(0.25) = 12.04 dB in amplitude level. If the same algorithm assigns G=1 to a drum transient, the transient remains unchanged in this simplified model. A listener could then hear a percussion-forward performance that Paul did not actually play. This numerical example is not a measurement of any algorithm or KLAAT recording. Real enhancement systems may also modify phase, create nonlinear artifacts, or reconstruct rather than merely attenuate components.


WHAT IS ESTABLISHED


Digital noise suppression and speech enhancement are real categories of audio processing. The WebRTC Audio Processing Module includes voice-communication processing facilities, including noise suppression; RNNoise is an open-source recurrent-neural-network noise-suppression implementation. Their existence establishes that such processing can occur, not that it occurred in Paul's clips. Time-frequency gain modification mathematically changes spectral balance when gains differ between bands or moments. A processed signal is therefore not automatically an acoustically faithful record of the input.


WHAT IS CONTEXT-DEPENDENT


Whether the drone is preferentially suppressed depends on its harmonic and noise content, microphone placement, competing sounds, algorithm, app mode, gain controls and processing history. A drum attack might be treated as a transient noise and attenuated rather than preserved. Some systems detect speech activity and apply stronger suppression in speech gaps; others behave differently. A human listener's judgment of a recording may also be influenced by speaker response and listening level. There is no justified single prediction for KLAAT without a device-specific test.


WHAT IS PLAUSIBLE BUT UNTESTED FOR KLAAT


A speech-enhanced recording might make Amastacia sound thinner or intermittently gated while Ankora appears unusually prominent. That could distort judgments about Paul's breath-to-drum coordination, the apparent density of the drone, and the overall continuity of the improvisation. Conversely, suppressing drum transients could make a naturally active passage appear smoother. Either change could affect listener ratings of energy, groove, or absorption, but no KLAAT-specific listening study has established such an effect.


WHAT WOULD BE AN UNSUPPORTED CLAIM


It would be unsupported to say that a particular KLAAT phone video definitely used voice isolation; that all noise suppression removes didgeridoo drones; that one processing artifact explains a listener's trance-like experience; or that preserved low-frequency energy demonstrates neural entrainment. The artist's felt flow, the listener's report, the recorded waveform, and the processor's output are separate observations.


HOW TO TEST THE CAPTURE PATH WITHOUT REWRITING THE MUSIC


The cleanest comparison records the same naturally improvised passage simultaneously through two documented capture paths: a minimally processed original and a path with known speech enhancement. Simultaneous capture matters because two separate performances are never identical. Keep microphones as close in position and orientation as feasible, avoid clipping, and document sample rates, app versions, processing settings and any automatic gain control. Time-align the files using recognizable transients; compare loudness-matched versions and retain the untouched originals.


Measure low-band and harmonic-band energy in sections where Amastacia is prominent, and separately inspect Ankora attack envelopes and decays. A difference in band energy after level matching is an acoustic observation. Audible 'pumping' or intermittent drone disappearance is a perceptual observation. The attribution to a particular algorithm is a causal hypothesis requiring controlled settings or additional evidence. Do not use an automatic harmonic/percussive separator as the sole ground truth: Session 157 showed why complementary source estimates can create misleading anticorrelation.


A useful quantitative metric is the change in drone-to-drum energy ratio between paths, in decibels. Let R_raw = 10 log10(E_drone,raw/E_drum,raw) and R_processed similarly, with carefully defined time-frequency regions and identical calibration conventions. The difference R_processed - R_raw can summarize a relative balance shift. It does not independently identify which component changed or why. Because the instruments overlap spectrally, the labels 'drone' and 'drum' are operational analysis regions, not perfect isolated sources. Report uncertainties and listen to the raw waveform alongside the metric.


A listening comparison could then present level-matched, anonymized A/B clips to volunteers, with randomized order and neutral questions: Which seems to have more continuous low sound? Which feels more rhythmically forceful? Which would you choose for attentive listening or an everyday soundtrack? These are subjective outcomes, not biomarkers. Participants should be allowed to say 'no difference.' If processing is audible, perfect blinding may be impossible; this limitation belongs in the record.


WHY THE ARTISTIC IDENTITY MATTERS


KLAAT's power as an artistic world is not a requirement that every performance be 'optimized' to a measurement. The point is to protect the evidence of what actually happened when breath, wood-like agave resonance, skin percussion and bodily movement met in a spontaneous moment. A processor that assumes 'speech foreground, everything else background' may be particularly ill-suited to an art form in which the sustained non-speech sound is itself the foreground. That mismatch is a technical question, not an invitation to simplify the music for an algorithm.


UNRESOLVED QUESTIONS


Does Paul's current phone record ordinary camera audio, enhanced voice audio, or a mixture depending on app? Are any existing shared clips derived from video-call captures rather than the camera's original file? Do particular processing settings alter the low drone more than the drum decay? Are the differences audible on ordinary speakers, or only measurable under controlled conditions? Does processing change enjoyment, activation or absorption for unfamiliar listeners? These remain open until KLAAT-specific files and listeners are tested.


EVIDENCE REGISTER


Established: Speech-oriented noise suppression exists; selective time-frequency attenuation changes a signal's spectral balance; a processed file need not represent the original acoustic event faithfully.
Context-dependent: The direction, size and audibility of any alteration vary with implementation, capture environment and playback.
Plausible/testable: Voice processing could change the apparent Amastacia–Ankora balance and hence listener descriptions.
Speculative: Such changes might influence reported immersion or everyday activation.
Unsupported: Any claim that KLAAT has demonstrated trance induction, cardiac or neural entrainment, therapeutic efficacy, or a special neurological effect from its C# drone.
