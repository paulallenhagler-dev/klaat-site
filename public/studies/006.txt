SESSION 6 — BREATH IS A BRIDGE TO THE AUTONOMIC NERVOUS SYSTEM
ARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT VERBATIM
Core lesson
Breathing is unusual because it is both automatic and voluntarily controllable. It interacts with heart-rate variability, blood-pressure regulation, vagal influences, and attention. Slow breathing near roughly 4.5–6.5 breaths per minute often strengthens cardiorespiratory oscillations in many adults, but there is no universal magic rate.


For KLAAT, the important question is whether the performance alters breathing without explicit instruction. That could provide one plausible pathway from sound and attention to broader physiological change.


Evidence boundary
Established: respiration affects heart-rate dynamics and autonomic measures. Supported but individual: slow breathing often increases certain HRV measures and baroreflex-related oscillations. Unsupported: claiming KLAAT automatically “activates the vagus nerve” in a specific therapeutic way.


KLAAT test
Use a sequence of silent baseline, drone, drone-plus-pulse, return to drone, then silence. Count visible breaths or use a consenting participant’s self-count and compare breathing rate across phases.
