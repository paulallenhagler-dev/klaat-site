RESPIRATORY ENTRAINMENT: CAN DRONE CHANGE BREATHING?

Independent research session from the KLAAT Resonance Research corpus.

SESSION 019 / 164Drone & BreathARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION · NOT VERBATIM

This early session is an archival reconstruction, not a verbatim transcript. Its status is preserved exactly as marked in the source corpus.

Core lesson A change in breathing rate is not automatically entrainment. True entrainment requires a reproducible temporal relationship between the stimulus and respiration, potentially including phase locking or a stable n:m ratio.

Amastacia may influence breathing through phrasing, attention, relaxation, activation, or imitation of performer breath. Ankora provides a separate rhythmic reference. These effects need to be distinguished.

Evidence boundary Established: auditory rhythm and attention can influence behavior and timing; breathing rate is modifiable by context. Plausible: some listeners may stabilize breathing relative to KLAAT timing. Unsupported: claiming that a listener has “entrained” merely because respiration slowed.

KLAAT test Record two minutes of baseline breathing followed by a KLAAT passage. Mark inhalation onsets and Ankora strikes, then look for stable relationships across time rather than simply comparing average breaths per minute.
