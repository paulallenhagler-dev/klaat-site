SESSION 17 — ORIENTING RESPONSE: CHANGE DETECTION
ARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT VERBATIM


Core lesson
The orienting response is the organism's shift of attention toward something novel, significant, or unexpected. It is not the same as the startle reflex. A subtle change in timbre, space, rhythm, or silence can orient attention without being loud or frightening.


Measures associated with orienting can include electrodermal activity, heart-rate changes, pupil changes, and altered respiration, but none of those alone tell us what emotion the listener felt.


Evidence boundary
Established: novel and behaviorally relevant stimuli can trigger orienting responses and measurable autonomic changes. Established: electrodermal activity reflects sympathetic sudomotor activity, not a direct readout of fear. Plausible: KLAAT can use controlled novelty as a Threshold Gate event.


KLAAT test
Hold a stable Amastacia drone for about five minutes. Introduce four short, non-louder timbral deviations. With consent, video small posture or head-orientation changes and compare them with listeners’ later recall of the events.
