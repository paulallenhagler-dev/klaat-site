Core lesson The orienting response becomes especially useful in KLAAT when placed inside a long period of repetition. Repetition establishes a prediction; a small deviation, omission, or subtraction can then trigger renewed attention without requiring a dramatic increase in volume.

Novelty can alter electrodermal activity, heart rate, respiration, and attention, but those changes should not be reduced to one emotion such as fear or calm.

Evidence boundary Established: repeated stimuli can lose orienting power while novel or changed stimuli restore attention. Established: silence itself can become salient when it violates a strong expectation. Plausible: KLAAT's Threshold Gate can be understood partly as an orienting transition.

KLAAT test At approximately the normal ~137-BPM pulse, maintain a stable pattern and compare the effect of omitting one beat versus omitting four consecutive beats. Ask which interruption produced the larger attentional reset.
