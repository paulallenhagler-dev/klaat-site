SESSION 22 — AUDITORY MASKING
ARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT VERBATIM


Core lesson
One sound can make another harder to hear. Simultaneous masking occurs when sounds overlap; forward masking can briefly affect perception after the masker stops. Auditory filters and spectral proximity strongly influence how much masking occurs.


The drum can therefore temporarily hide details in the didgeridoo, and the didgeridoo can obscure drum components. More layers do not necessarily reveal more information.


Evidence boundary
Established: auditory masking is a core property of hearing and depends on spectrum, level, and timing. Plausible: KLAAT can deliberately compose mask-and-reveal transitions. Unsupported: assuming spectral complexity always increases immersion.


KLAAT test
Use the same the didgeridoo recording beneath soft, moderate, and strong the drum overdubs. Ask listeners to track one specific harmonic or overtone and identify the point at which it disappears and reappears.
