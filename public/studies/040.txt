SESSION 40 — SENSORY ADAPTATION & CONTRAST: REMOVING SOUND CAN FEEL LIKE ADDING
ARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT VERBATIM


Core lesson
A stable Amastacia drone establishes a sensory baseline. Once that baseline is learned, stopping the sound can become a major perceptual event. Silence after ten minutes of drone is therefore not the same perceptual state as silence before the drone.


Duration changes the listener through adaptation, expectation, attention, breathing, and time perception. A stimulus multiplied by time is not simply “more of the same.”


Evidence boundary
Established: adaptation and contrast alter sensory experience. Plausible: prolonged KLAAT exposure can make later subtraction feel more salient than an added sound. Unsupported: claiming the post-sound state has a specific therapeutic meaning without evidence.


KLAAT test
Compare 30 seconds of drone followed by 60 seconds of silence with 10 minutes of drone followed by 60 seconds of silence. Use neutral reports and compare how intense or unusual the silence feels.
