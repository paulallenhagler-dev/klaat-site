SESSION 10 — ROUGHNESS, DISSONANCE & SPECTRAL TENSION
ARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT VERBATIM
Core lesson
When frequency components fall close enough together within auditory filters, their interaction can produce beating and perceptual roughness. Roughness contributes to urgency, tension, and sensory salience without requiring faster tempo or higher overall volume.


Amastacia can potentially control roughness through vocalization, embouchure, overtone structure, and spectral instability. This makes roughness a separate compositional axis from loudness or BPM.


Evidence boundary
Established: close spectral interactions and rapid amplitude fluctuations contribute to roughness and aspects of sensory dissonance. Supported but not universal: rougher sounds can feel more urgent or aversive. Unsupported: calling any roughness band a universal “fear frequency.”


KLAAT test
Record three matched-loudness Amastacia passages: spectrally stable, moderately rough, and intentionally rough. Compare listener ratings for tension, activation, urgency, and loudness.
