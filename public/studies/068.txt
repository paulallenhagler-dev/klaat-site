THE BODY IS NOT A MIXER
Multi-Input Vibration, Cross-Axis Coupling, and the Limits of Adding Up What Listeners Feel

CENTRAL CLAIM TO TEST


THE VIBRATION REACHING A LISTENER THROUGH THE SEAT, FEET AND BACK CANNOT BE CHARACTERIZED RELIABLY BY ASSUMING THAT THREE SEPARATE SENSOR READINGS ARE THREE INDEPENDENT BODILY EXPERIENCES.


This is a warning about measurement and inference, not a claim that every KLAAT performance produces important whole-body vibration. It is also not a claim that linear addition is always mathematically wrong. A linear, time-invariant system with adequately characterized inputs can obey superposition. The problem is that occupied human/support systems can couple axes and interfaces, change with posture and excitation magnitude, and sometimes behave nonlinearly. We must first establish which of these complications matter at actual KLAAT levels.


1. WHY RETURN TO THE BODY NOW?


Sessions 63–67 developed temporal grammar, blind architecture validation, a provisional State Vector, and the distinction between event urgency and perceived beat speed. Those are important. But the accumulated hourly work exposes a different unresolved problem: KLAAT repeatedly speaks about the listener's body as though the delivered mechanical field were straightforward to measure. It is not.


Session 60 established the listener-plus-support as a loaded mechanical system. Session 61 showed that the airborne room field differs by position. Session 62 separated low-frequency whole-body behavior from higher-frequency tactile-interface mechanisms. The October 5, 18:33 hourly note then asked a sharper question: can separate seat, foot and back measurements predict the response when all interfaces are excited simultaneously, or do multi-input coupling and nonlinear interactions defeat that shortcut?


This is a high-value correction because a wrong mechanical model can make every later claim about bodily impact, activation or venue design look more precise than it is.


2. THREE DISTINCT THINGS THAT MUST NOT BE COLLAPSED


SOURCE: vibration generated by Amastacia, Ankora, loudspeakers used for research playback, and room/support structures.


INTERFACE INPUT: motion at the actual contact surfaces: seat pan, backrest, floor/foot support, and relevant axes.


RESPONSE: motion, force or perception associated with the occupied body/support system.


A sensor on a wooden floor measures floor acceleration at its mounting point. It does not measure vibration dose in a person's body. A seat-frame sensor does not necessarily measure motion at the pelvis. A large contact acceleration does not prove that pathway dominates the listener's experience.


For KLAAT, these must be recorded as different levels of the chain: PRODUCED → DELIVERED AT CONTACT → BODY/SUPPORT RESPONSE → PERCEIVED.


3. WHAT CROSS-AXIS COUPLING MEANS


Imagine a vertical vibration input at a seat. The seated body can also produce fore-aft force or motion. The axes are mechanically coupled by posture, tissue, joints, seat geometry and support constraints.


That is cross-axis response. It is not an exotic energy transfer, a new frequency, or proof of therapeutic resonance.


Qiu and Griffin (2012) directly observed cross-axis apparent-mass behavior during controlled vertical and fore-aft vibration. Their study also found magnitude-dependent nonlinear behavior and effects of the backrest. These are controlled human biodynamics results, not measurements of KLAAT.


4. WHAT MULTI-INPUT COUPLING MEANS


Now add multiple contact inputs: seat, backrest, feet, perhaps in multiple axes. The response to any one input may depend on what the other inputs are doing.


A multi-input/single-output (MISO) model estimates one response from several synchronized input channels. A single-input/single-output (SISO) model uses only one.


Wu, Qiu and Zhou (2022) studied twelve participants under single-axis and combined vertical, lateral and roll excitations. They found MISO estimation more suitable for the multi-input apparent-mass problem than SISO estimation in their studied conditions.


The important lesson is not “MISO is always better.” It is that when several inputs are active, omitting them can misattribute the response to whichever sensor happens to be nearest or largest.


5. LINEAR SUPERPOSITION IS NOT THE ENEMY


There are two separate reasons a one-sensor or simple-sum explanation can fail.


FIRST: a system can be linear but MULTI-INPUT. Several inputs each contribute through different transfer functions. You still need to measure the relevant inputs, their timing and their frequency-dependent paths.


SECOND: a system can be NONLINEAR or change with conditions. The transfer behavior may differ with excitation magnitude, posture, contact or muscle tension. A model calibrated in one condition may fail in another.


Do not conflate these. MISO outperforming SISO does not by itself demonstrate nonlinearity. Likewise cross-axis coupling can exist in a linear system.


6. WHY SUMMING ACCELEROMETERS IS A CATEGORY ERROR


Adding the numeric RMS accelerations at seat, feet and backrest is not a valid direct measure of a listener's total felt vibration.


The readings are at different contact points, potentially along different axes, with different frequency responses and body transfer paths. Even if a formal standard uses frequency weighting or vector combination for a specific exposure assessment, that does not turn a raw sum into an embodied-perception score.


The correct starting point is synchronized, axis-labeled, location-labeled measurements. Any aggregate must state what it represents and which model justifies it.


7. POSTURE IS PART OF THE TRANSFER FUNCTION


The same room and same instruments can reach two seated listeners differently because their feet, backs and pelvis contact different surfaces under different loads.


Fairley and Griffin (1989) found that footrest, backrest, posture, muscle tension and vibration magnitude can alter apparent-mass behavior, with considerable intersubject variability.


A 2026 study by Yin, Qiu, Liu and Zheng tested different backrest angles and vibration magnitudes. It found a significant interaction for fore-aft resonance parameters, not a universal interaction across every axis.


Thus a posture effect is plausible for KLAAT; its direction and magnitude cannot be imported from vehicle-vibration studies.


8. THE FREQUENCY-RANGE VALIDITY GATE


Most of the strongest whole-body biodynamics studies concern controlled excitation in roughly the 0.2–20 Hz region, often at magnitudes relevant to ride comfort or occupational exposure.


KLAAT may deliver some energy there, but Ankora's nominal ~137 BPM is approximately 2.28 rhythmic events per second—not proof of a 2.28 Hz mechanical vibration carrier. An Ankora strike is a transient with a broad spectrum; Amastacia's acoustic drone occupies its own frequency range. Event repetition frequency, sound frequency and structure-borne acceleration spectra must be kept separate.


Higher-frequency tactile energy, if present at a contact surface, may be governed by different pathways and perceptual mechanisms. We cannot transfer low-frequency whole-body coefficients into those bands merely because both are called vibration.


9. BEFORE MULTI-INPUT MODELING, PROVE THERE IS AN INPUT


An intellectually necessary possibility: in an ordinary live KLAAT venue, Amastacia and Ankora may create compelling airborne sound but little measurable structure-borne motion at the listener's seat or floor.


The experiment must begin with a noise-floor and baseline check.


If mechanical signals are below useful measurement resolution, stop claiming a mechanical pathway for that configuration. The perceived bodily quality may instead be predominantly auditory, perceptual, or produced through other routes.


A complex multi-input model of measurement noise is worse than a simple honest null result.


10. AMASTACIA AND ANKORA MAY DRIVE DIFFERENT PATHWAYS


Amastacia produces sustained acoustic energy and breath-shaped fluctuations. Ankora produces discrete impacts and decaying transients. These different envelopes can make system identification easier if they provide distinguishable input signatures.


But this is not guaranteed. The two instruments may overlap spectrally or excite common structural modes. Room acoustics can also couple their airborne contributions at a listener's position.


A useful pilot therefore records Amastacia alone, Ankora alone, and both together, while preserving realistic performance levels. That tests the instrumentation and the physical transfer problem; it does not mean the actual KLAAT performance should be decomposed into artificial tracks.


11. THE FOUR-PHASE ARCHITECTURE AS A MECHANICAL QUESTION


GROUND ANCHOR — Amastacia establishes the airborne Field. Ask whether any contact pathway is measurable and stable or whether the Field is mainly airborne at that listener position.


THRESHOLD GATE — Ankora begins discrete impacts. Ask whether a new contact response appears, whether it is seat-, foot- or back-dominant, and whether the physical onset matches the perceived Gate. They need not coincide.


RISING PULSE — Ankora's Surface Pressure changes. Ask whether mechanical event magnitude, cross-axis response or contact-pathway weighting changes while the Scaffold BPM remains stable. Do not infer more bodily impact simply from higher event density.


RETURN PATH — Surface complexity and then the Clock withdraw. Ask whether mechanically measured events vanish before the listener's reported bodily afterstate or internalized beat. Persistent sensation does not prove persistent mechanical energy.


12. HOW THIS RELATES TO KSV-0.1


The provisional State Vector remains [FIELD, CLOCK, SURFACE, WITHDRAWAL]. The new multi-input measurements do not automatically become a fifth state variable.


They are currently DELIVERY/RECEPTION DIAGNOSTICS and possible moderators of the relationship between delivered musical state and perceived bodily impact.


If a particular contact-pathway pattern predicts phase perception or bodily salience beyond acoustic level and KSV in replicated data, it might deserve promotion. Until then, keep physical measurements and perceptual outcomes separate.


13. THE RIGHT MODEL COMPARISON


Use nested explanations rather than jumping to a sophisticated model:


M0: noise floor / baseline; is there a reliable mechanical signal?
M1: strongest single interface/axis input predicts a separate response channel.
M2: multiple synchronized interface/axis inputs predict that response linearly.
M3: the multi-input model plus a small, pre-specified set of interaction or nonlinear terms.


A valid M2 model can be sufficient. M3 should not win just because it has more parameters. Evaluate on held-out trials or a separate performance. Report uncertainty, residual error and whether the gain is large enough to matter in practice.


14. IDENTIFIABILITY: THE INPUTS MUST DIFFER ENOUGH


A critical trap: if seat, feet and backrest all move together in nearly identical patterns, a fitted model may not be able to determine which pathway is responsible.


This is collinearity, not evidence that all three pathways are equally important.


An isolated-path characterization may help where it is physically possible and safe, but changing contact also changes the boundary conditions. A single-input test under a different posture cannot automatically be summed into the natural multi-input condition.


Keep both kinds of data: characterization trials and ecological KLAAT trials. Do not confuse them.


15. WHAT SENSORS CAN AND CANNOT TELL US


A tri-axial accelerometer reports local acceleration along its mounted axes, subject to calibration, mounting and noise limits. It does not measure force or apparent mass by itself.


Estimating apparent mass requires an appropriate force measurement together with acceleration and the relevant transfer-function analysis. A body-mounted motion sensor, if used, measures motion at its own site, not a universal bodily dose.


A microphone measures airborne pressure, not structural acceleration. These channels can be synchronized but must not be treated as interchangeable.


16. THE SAFETY AND STANDARDS BOUNDARY


ISO 2631-1 provides a formal framework for evaluating certain whole-body vibration exposures, including measurement locations at the seat, backrest and feet. It is not a validated scoring system for KLAAT's artistic effectiveness, trance, pleasure or healing.


The research should stay at ordinary, comfortable performance levels. Do not add powerful transducers, deliberately seek bodily resonance, or increase exposure simply to obtain a more dramatic sensor trace. Hearing exposure and mechanical exposure require their own appropriate precautions.


17. ESTABLISHED EVIDENCE


ESTABLISHED — Human seated-body biodynamics can show cross-axis coupling under controlled vibration.
ESTABLISHED — Apparent-mass and transmissibility behavior can depend on excitation magnitude, backrest, foot support and posture.
ESTABLISHED — Multi-input identification can outperform single-input estimation when multiple vibration inputs contribute to a measured response.
ESTABLISHED — The response of a linear multi-input system can obey superposition while still requiring several distinct transfer paths.
ESTABLISHED — A contact-surface accelerometer reading is not itself a measure of subjective bodily impact.
ESTABLISHED — Standardized whole-body vibration assessment distinguishes contact locations and measurement axes.


18. CONTEXT-DEPENDENT EVIDENCE


CONTEXT-DEPENDENT — Much of the cited literature uses 0.2–20 Hz controlled vibration and magnitudes unlike unknown live KLAAT exposure.
CONTEXT-DEPENDENT — The importance of seat, feet and backrest depends on the actual venue, furniture, body position and source-to-support coupling.
CONTEXT-DEPENDENT — MISO improvement is not proof of nonlinear behavior; nonlinearity must be tested separately.
CONTEXT-DEPENDENT — Mechanical motion at one interface may be physically measurable yet perceptually negligible.
CONTEXT-DEPENDENT — The model that best predicts a physical sensor may not best predict subjective bodily impact.
CONTEXT-DEPENDENT — Higher-frequency tactile effects require separate frequency-appropriate measurements and interpretation.


19. PLAUSIBLE / TESTABLE KLAAT HYPOTHESES


PLAUSIBLE/TESTABLE — In occupied KLAAT support configurations with measurable simultaneous multi-interface excitation, M2 will predict held-out response-channel motion better than M1.
PLAUSIBLE/TESTABLE — Some postures or contact conditions will alter cross-axis response enough to change the fitted transfer relationship.
PLAUSIBLE/TESTABLE — A limited M3 interaction model may improve prediction in some configurations, but not all.
PLAUSIBLE/TESTABLE — Ankora's transient-rich events may reveal contact pathways not evident during Amastacia's sustained Field.
PLAUSIBLE/TESTABLE — A measured mechanical Gate can differ in timing or strength from the perceived Threshold Gate.
PLAUSIBLE/TESTABLE — After accounting for airborne level and KSV, contact-pathway measurements may explain additional variance in bodily-impact ratings for some listener positions.


20. SPECULATION


SPECULATION — KLAAT may eventually identify one or two dominant support pathways in certain venues, enabling simpler measurement and seating design.
SPECULATION — A reproducible mechanical response pattern may become a controllable performance affordance, but only if it survives independent perceptual testing.
SPECULATION — Some of KLAAT's perceived “moving vibration” may reflect changing pathway weighting or multisensory inference rather than physical vibration traveling through the body.
SPECULATION — In some venues the strongest embodied effect may remain predominantly auditory despite weak contact acceleration.


21. UNSUPPORTED CLAIMS


UNSUPPORTED — Seat + feet + back acceleration values can be added to calculate “whole-body KLAAT dose.”
UNSUPPORTED — The largest accelerometer reading identifies the dominant perceptual pathway.
UNSUPPORTED — Cross-axis response proves a special resonance or healing mechanism.
UNSUPPORTED — A model with more inputs must be more scientifically correct or artistically valuable.
UNSUPPORTED — A better mechanical model guarantees greater immersion.
UNSUPPORTED — A 137 BPM drum pattern necessarily creates a 2.28 Hz body resonance.
UNSUPPORTED — Occupational whole-body vibration coefficients predict KLAAT effects without matching frequency, magnitude and boundary conditions.
UNSUPPORTED — Any mechanical response demonstrates trance, neural entrainment, therapeutic benefit or autonomic change.


22. METHODOLOGY UPDATE — THE MULTI-INPUT VALIDITY GATE


Add to KLAAT research protocol:


(A) State the physical quantity at every sensor: acceleration, force, sound pressure or a listener report.
(B) Record sensor location, axis, mounting, calibration, sampling rate and occupied/unoccupied condition.
(C) Separate the sound spectrum, mechanical spectrum and rhythmic event rate.
(D) Establish mechanical signal above noise floor before modeling pathways.
(E) Preserve synchronized multiple input channels and a separate response channel.
(F) Compare strongest-path M1, linear multi-input M2 and constrained interaction M3 on held-out data.
(G) Check collinearity/identifiability and report when pathways cannot be disentangled.
(H) Preserve posture, contact, support geometry and listener position.
(I) Treat objective mechanics and subjective bodily impact as different endpoints.
(J) Do not promote mechanical diagnostics into KSV unless they add replicated explanatory value.


The governing rule:


DO NOT CALL A MULTI-CONTACT BODY RESPONSE “ADDITIVE” OR “NONLINEAR” UNTIL THE MODEL AND THE DATA ACTUALLY DISTINGUISH THOSE POSSIBILITIES.


23. CORE KLAAT, WITHOUT A NEW INSTRUMENT


This entire problem belongs to the reception of Amastacia and Ankora. It does not authorize an added vibrotactile orchestra.


Amastacia remains the Drone / Field.
Ankora remains the Pulse / Event.
Breath determines human phrasing.
Repetition establishes expectation.
Silence exposes what is absent.
Duration allows adaptation and state trajectories.
Room and support structures shape delivery.
The listener's body and posture shape reception.


Microphones, accelerometers and statistical models are investigative instruments, not performing instruments.


24. WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR PRACTICE?


If the multi-input model materially outperforms the strongest single pathway at realistic KLAAT levels, venue mapping should preserve multiple interfaces rather than claiming one chair or floor reading represents the body.


If M1 performs just as well, simplify the measurement setup.


If mechanical signals are negligible, stop treating mechanical coupling as an important explanation for that venue; investigate airborne sound, expectation and multisensory perception instead.


If M3 offers no robust held-out improvement, do not invoke nonlinear body mechanics merely because the phrase sounds sophisticated.


These are useful outcomes in every direction.


25. KRX-068 — THE OCCUPIED MULTI-INPUT IDENTIFICATION TEST


GOAL: Determine whether realistic KLAAT support vibration, if measurable, requires more than one contact/axis input to predict a separate response channel.


SETUP: In one ordinary listening location, mount calibrated synchronized tri-axial accelerometers on the seat-pan contact region, foot support and backrest contact region where feasible. Use a fourth independent response sensor on a documented support/body-proxy location; if a body-worn sensor is used, obtain consent and specify the location. Record a microphone channel for airborne sound. Keep all exposures at normal comfortable performance levels. Do not use added vibration drivers.


BASELINE: Record quiet occupied and unoccupied conditions. Check instrument self-noise, mounting artifacts and whether any KLAAT signal rises reliably above the baseline.


PLAYED CONDITIONS: Human-perform (1) Amastacia-only Ground Anchor material; (2) sparse Ankora Threshold Gate material; (3) a natural Amastacia + Ankora Rising Pulse with fixed approximate Scaffold BPM and varied Surface; (4) a Return Path that withdraws Ankora and leaves Amastacia. Repeat the blocks with natural variation; preserve their actual event times.


CONTACT CONDITIONS: Where safe and comfortable, repeat with ordinary upright/back-contact and slight forward/no-back-contact positions, recording the changed posture. Do not pretend these conditions isolate a pathway without also changing body mechanics.


ANALYSIS: Predefine physically meaningful frequency bands according to the measured spectra and sensor response; do not equate the 137 BPM event rate with a mechanical carrier. Predict the independent response channel using M1 strongest single input, M2 synchronized linear multi-input, and M3 only a few pre-specified interactions if sample size and signal quality permit. Use held-out blocks, inspect residuals and collinearity, and report uncertainty. Compare the physical results with separate listener ratings of bodily impact and Pulse clarity, without defining one from the other.


DECISION RULE: If M2 consistently and meaningfully improves held-out prediction over M1, retain multi-interface characterization for that venue. If M3 adds repeatable gain, investigate nonlinearity in a dedicated follow-up. If no reliable mechanical signal is detected, document a null mechanical pathway rather than fitting noise.


ONE PRACTICAL QUESTION TO ANSWER:


WHEN AMASTACIA HOLDS THE FIELD AND ANKORA INTRODUCES THE PULSE, IS THE LISTENER'S MEASURABLE SUPPORT RESPONSE BEST EXPLAINED BY ONE DOMINANT CONTACT PATH, SEVERAL LINEARLY COUPLED PATHS, OR A GENUINELY CONDITION-DEPENDENT INTERACTION?


That answer—not the most dramatic sensor trace—determines whether multi-interface mechanics deserve a permanent place in KLAAT's venue methodology.
