KLAAT / RESONANCE RESEARCH / SESSION 053

THE PREDICTION BUDGET: How KLAAT Can Escalate Without Becoming Louder, Denser, or Random

A contrast stops functioning as a true surprise if the listener learns that the contrast itself is coming.

SESSION 053 / 164Listening & PerceptionRESEARCH SESSION

Session 52 introduced tactile negative space: Amastacia can potentially make Ankora more bodily legible by yielding sensory space before selected Pulse events. The newest hourly work reveals the danger in turning that into a trick.

A contrast stops functioning as a true surprise if the listener learns that the contrast itself is coming.

That gives KLAAT a deeper compositional problem. Rising Pulse needs enough regularity for the listener to form a model, but not so much repetition that every important deviation becomes part of that model. It also needs enough deviation to renew attention without destroying the Pulse that makes deviation meaningful in the first place.

This session develops a KLAAT-specific construct for that problem:

PREDICTION BUDGET.

Prediction Budget is not a standard neuroscience quantity. It is a KLAAT compositional model for managing the relationship between stable expectation and meaningful violation across time.

The core principle is:

A VIOLATION ONLY HAS FORCE WHILE THERE IS A MODEL LEFT TO VIOLATE.

1. A DEVIANT CAN BECOME THE NEW NORMAL

Auditory research strongly supports the underlying mechanism. Repeated sounds commonly produce response attenuation, but response depends on more than physical repetition. Probability, context, timing, attention, and learned sequence structure matter. Auditory systems learn regularities over multiple timescales.

Most important for KLAAT, a "deviant" is not permanently deviant.

If an unusual event begins occurring reliably, listeners can learn the higher-order regularity governing that event. Research on repeated deviants and statistical learning shows that events initially functioning as violations can themselves become predictive.

This immediately changes how we should treat techniques such as a pre-Ankora Amastacia dip, displaced accent, omitted strike, recurring density burst, spectral opening, or delayed entrance. None is intrinsically surprising. Its effect depends on history.

If Amastacia ducks before every fourth Ankora strike for several minutes, the duck may stop functioning as negative space that renews the Pulse and become part of the Pulse itself. That is not necessarily bad. It means the device has changed function: VIOLATION to RULE.

2. KLAAT NEEDS HIERARCHICAL EXPECTATION

LEVEL 1 — PULSE: the listener learns the basic Ankora temporal scaffold. LEVEL 2 — CONTRAST MOTIF: a recurring event appears against that scaffold. LEVEL 3 — CONTRAST RULE: the listener begins to learn when or how that motif tends to occur. LEVEL 4 — RULE CHANGE: KLAAT alters probability, family, placement, or governing logic while preserving enough lower-level scaffold for the change to remain intelligible.

The sequence becomes: LEARN THE PULSE → LEARN THE CONTRAST → CHANGE THE RULE.

That is fundamentally different from: PLAY MORE → HIT HARDER → GET LOUDER.

3. WHY RANDOMNESS IS NOT THE ANSWER

If predictability can reduce surprise, maximizing unpredictability sounds tempting. That would be a mistake. Recent groove and predictive-timing work supports a more nuanced picture: in familiar rhythmic contexts, urge-to-move often peaks at intermediate rather than maximal complexity, but the effect depends on meter, musical context, listener experience, and task. There is no universal optimal syncopation value.

The useful KLAAT lesson is: PREDICTION ERROR NEEDS PREDICTION.

If Ankora becomes so irregular that the listener cannot infer a temporal scaffold, an omission may cease to feel like an omission. If Amastacia changes continuously, a spectral opening may no longer feel like an opening because there was no stable field from which it departed.

KLAAT is not searching for maximum surprise. It is searching for CONTROLLED LEGIBILITY.

4. THE PREDICTION BUDGET

Prediction Budget describes how much local unpredictability a passage can support while preserving a sufficiently strong higher-order model. It is a compositional balance, not a numerical meter.

When the model is extremely stable, KLAAT has room to introduce a strong violation. When several violations accumulate, the system should periodically reconfirm the scaffold.

If one contrast device becomes expected, KLAAT can allow it to become a motif; change its probability; change its placement; change the event family; or change the rule governing several contrast families.

Escalation can occur by moving UP THE HIERARCHY rather than increasing the magnitude of the same event.

5. AMASTACIA'S ROLE: DEFINE WHAT COUNTS AS STABLE

Amastacia can provide continuity while Ankora's local grammar changes: spectral identity, room field, breath continuity, mechanical background, and the sense that the listener remains inside one acoustic world.

But Amastacia can also participate in the prediction hierarchy. A recurring breath articulation can become expected. A spectral opening can become a motif. A pre-Pulse reduction can become a cue. Sudden absence can become a rule-level event.

Amastacia can move between FIELD, CUE, and EVENT.

6. ANKORA'S ROLE: PRESERVE THE CLOCK WHILE CHANGING THE GRAMMAR

At the provisional ~137 BPM reference, Ankora can maintain a temporal scaffold while local events change. The Pulse can say THIS IS STILL THE SAME WORLD while the contrast grammar says BUT THE RULES INSIDE IT ARE CHANGING.

7. DISHABITUATION IS NOT A UNIVERSAL RESET BUTTON

Changed stimuli can restore orienting or neural response after repetition, but evidence does not support a simple "novel sound resets habituation" rule. Changing acoustic identity, spatial location, timing, probability, or higher-order sequence structure can engage different representations. KLAAT should ask WHAT DIMENSION HAS BECOME EXPECTED? and WHAT LEVEL OF THE MODEL ARE WE CHANGING?

8. THE FOUR-PHASE ARCHITECTURE BECOMES A PREDICTION ARC

GROUND ANCHOR — MAXIMIZE MODEL CERTAINTY. Establish what normal means.

THRESHOLD GATE — INTRODUCE A LEGIBLE VIOLATION. Make a meaningful exception coherent with the established world.

RISING PULSE — CLIMB THE PREDICTION HIERARCHY. Progress through stable Pulse, recognizable contrast motif, variation in motif probability, multiple contrast families, rule change, and strategic return to an expected anchor. Cycle CERTAINTY → VIOLATION → LEARNING → RULE CHANGE → RECONFIRMATION.

RETURN PATH — COLLAPSE THE HIERARCHY. Remove higher-order complexity, restore predictable anchors, let Ankora simplify or vanish, and allow Amastacia to reclaim the field.

9. WHY THIS IS KLAAT

KLAAT uses very few primary materials: one Drone, one Pulse, breath, room, body, duration. With such a sparse palette, repetition can become the mechanism by which tiny changes acquire enormous meaning. Sparse instrumentation becomes an advantage because the listener can learn the system deeply enough for small deviations to matter.

KLAAT does not need hundreds of musical events. It needs events with HISTORY.

10. EVIDENCE BOUNDARY

ESTABLISHED: Auditory responses and perception are shaped by repetition, event probability, temporal regularity, context, attention, and presentation history.

ESTABLISHED: Repeated deviants can themselves become predictable when they occur according to learnable higher-order statistics.

ESTABLISHED: Auditory response recovery after a changed stimulus is feature- and context-dependent; different change dimensions need not reflect one generic reset mechanism.

ESTABLISHED: Rhythmic engagement depends on the relationship between regularity and complexity rather than simply increasing with unpredictability; some groove studies show an inverted-U under particular conditions.

CONTEXT-DEPENDENT: The time required for a KLAAT contrast device to become expected is unknown and depends on event probability, rate, similarity, attention, prior musical experience, sequence complexity, room context, and listener.

CONTEXT-DEPENDENT: Groove findings cannot prescribe an optimal syncopation, complexity, or surprise percentage for KLAAT.

PLAUSIBLE / TESTABLE: A stable Ankora scaffold with multiple sparse, learnable contrast families will sustain activation and Pulse clarity longer than repeatedly using one identical contrast device.

PLAUSIBLE / TESTABLE: Changing the higher-order rule governing familiar contrast events can restore perceptual salience without increasing SPL, tempo, or event count.

PLAUSIBLE / TESTABLE: Strategic returns to fully expected anchor events will preserve the predictive scaffold and make subsequent violations more legible.

SPECULATION: The four KLAAT phases naturally correspond to increasing and then collapsing levels of predictive hierarchy.

SPECULATION: A listener will experience rule-level changes as more activating than equally large feature-level changes.

UNSUPPORTED: There is a fixed number of repetitions after which a KLAAT event becomes boring or habituated.

UNSUPPORTED: Moderate syncopation is universally optimal.

UNSUPPORTED: Randomness prevents habituation.

UNSUPPORTED: A larger EEG entrainment response means deeper KLAAT engagement.

UNSUPPORTED: A novel event automatically resets the listener's nervous system.

11. KLAAT METHODOLOGY UPDATE

Log SCAFFOLD, CONTRAST FAMILY, PROBABILITY, PLACEMENT, RULE, EXPOSURE, and RECOVERY.

New rules: DO NOT CONFUSE NOVELTY WITH RANDOMNESS. LET A RULE BECOME LEGIBLE BEFORE YOU BREAK IT. WHEN A CONTRAST BECOMES THE RULE, CHANGE THE LEVEL OF THE RULE — NOT JUST ITS VOLUME.

12. PRACTICAL EXPERIMENT — KRX-053: THE PREDICTION-BUDGET TEST

Build four six-minute Amastacia/Ankora passages. Keep approximate SPL, duration, Pulse rate, total Ankora strike count, and broad spectral content as similar as practical.

A — STABLE ANCHOR: highly regular Ankora scaffold with minimal contrast. B — SINGLE TRICK: one repeated contrast family at a stable location. C — HIERARCHICAL CONTRAST: same underlying Pulse, two or three contrast families according to a learnable higher-order rule; allow the rule to establish, then change it once at a preregistered point. D — HIGH UNCERTAINTY: similar contrast count, but both placement and family are difficult to predict.

Randomize passage order. Every 45–60 seconds collect activation, Pulse clarity, surprise, bodily impact, urge to move, and confidence about what will happen next. At the end ask whether the listener noticed a pattern in when unusual events happened.

Plot each measure across TIME.

Ask whether B loses surprise/impact; C preserves Pulse clarity while renewing salience at the rule change; D produces surprise but weakens coherent Pulse; and C outperforms B and D because it preserves a model while changing what the model predicts.

If C does, Prediction Budget becomes a directly useful KLAAT composition principle. If D performs equally well or better, the hierarchy hypothesis weakens. If B does not decay, individual contrast devices may remain useful longer than expected.

RECOMMENDED NEXT DIRECTION

The hourly research continued into electrodermal measurement, deconvolution, sensor standards, and candidate EDA hardware. That work is valuable experimental infrastructure, but it should not become the next numbered KLAAT lesson merely because it is technically sophisticated.

The strongest unresolved KLAAT direction is now: WHAT MAKES A RULE-LEVEL CHANGE FEEL LIKE A THRESHOLD RATHER THAN MERELY A VARIATION?

That connects predictive hierarchy directly to Threshold Gate and may let KLAAT define a Gate operationally: not by a particular sound, but by a sufficiently consequential change in the listener's model of what the performance is doing.

The next numbered session should proceed there only if the hourly research produces evidence strong enough to distinguish threshold formation from generic novelty
