KLAAT / RESONANCE RESEARCH / SESSION 063

THE INTERNAL CLOCK: Spontaneous Motor Tempo, Ankora’s ~137 BPM, and Why One Pulse Cannot Be “Natural” for Everyone

The governing principle is:

SESSION 063 / 164Pulse & RhythmRESEARCH SESSION

Session 62 established a Frequency-Range Gate for bodily vibration. The strongest unresolved direction still sitting in the Research Ledger is different: Ankora’s approximately 137 BPM reference has repeatedly been treated as a calibration anchor, but KLAAT has not yet asked how that external Pulse relates to the listener’s own preferred movement tempo.

This matters because sensorimotor synchronization is not imposed on a blank system. Humans show spontaneous motor tempo (SMT): a preferred rate of regular self-generated movement, commonly measured by comfortable finger tapping. SMT varies substantially across people and can also vary within a person with task, effector, age, arousal, time/context, and measurement procedure.

The governing principle is:

ANKORA HAS A TEMPO. THE LISTENER ALSO ARRIVES WITH ONE.

KLAAT therefore should not ask whether 137 BPM is “the entrainment frequency.” It should ask how the relationship between Ankora’s Pulse and a listener’s current endogenous timing tendency changes synchronization, movement, prediction, bodily impact, and phase perception.

1. 137 BPM IS A PERFORMANCE ANCHOR, NOT A HUMAN CONSTANT

The current KLAAT reference recording places Ankora around 137 BPM, approximately 438 ms between quarter-note-level events.

That sits comfortably inside a broad tempo region in which humans commonly perceive and synchronize to musical beats. But population-level comfort does not make 137 BPM universally optimal.

Spontaneous motor tempo varies widely across individuals. Large-sample and review work rejects the idea that every healthy adult possesses one canonical ~600 ms internal period.

Therefore:

137 BPM CAN BE A KLAAT IDENTITY WITHOUT BEING A BIOLOGICAL OPTIMUM.

This is artistically liberating. KLAAT does not need to chase an average human tempo. It needs to understand what its chosen Pulse does relative to different listeners.

2. SMT IS A RANGE AND A STATE, NOT A SOUL FREQUENCY

SMT is usually measured by asking a participant to tap regularly at the rate that feels most comfortable or natural.

It is useful.

It is not a hidden personal frequency.

Systematic review evidence shows both inter-individual and intra-individual variation. Measurement depends on instructions, repeated trials, effector, current state, and context.

Large-scale work also finds relationships with factors such as age and arousal.

So KLAAT must avoid turning SMT into another mystical number.

A listener’s measured SMT is:

A BEHAVIORAL REFERENCE UNDER SPECIFIED CONDITIONS.

Nothing more.

3. PREFERRED TEMPO AND PRODUCED TEMPO ARE RELATED

Research indicates that spontaneous motor tempo and preferred perceptual/music tempo are related within individuals.

People do not merely hear tempo abstractly. Their own motor timing tendencies contribute to which rates feel comfortable or preferred.

Passive listening near an individual’s preferred tempo has also been shown to modulate corticospinal excitability, although critically the direction of that modulation differed across participants.

That last result matters.

Even when tempo proximity affects the motor system, “closer to preferred tempo” does not imply one universal increase in activation.

KLAAT should expect listener-specific response shapes.

4. SYNCHRONIZATION DEPENDS ON THE RELATIONSHIP, NOT JUST THE BPM

Intrinsic rhythmicity and SMT can predict aspects of externally paced synchronization.

This suggests a more useful KLAAT variable than raw Ankora BPM:

TEMPO RELATIONSHIP = ANKORA PERIOD / LISTENER SMT PERIOD.

A listener whose spontaneous tapping sits close to Ankora may interact with the Pulse differently from someone whose spontaneous tempo is substantially slower or faster.

But proximity is not automatically better.

It may improve synchronization stability. It may increase ease. It may reduce effort. It may increase preference. Or, in KLAAT, a controlled mismatch may create useful tension.

Those are separate outcomes.

5. OCTAVE / METRIC EQUIVALENCE COMPLICATES EVERYTHING

Musical pulse is hierarchical.

A listener hearing Ankora at 137 BPM need not organize movement at 137 movements per minute.

They may nod or sway at approximately half-time. They may tap subdivisions. They may organize every second or fourth event into a larger cycle.

Therefore the relevant comparison cannot be:

SMT = 70 BPM, ANKORA = 137 BPM, THEREFORE LARGE MISMATCH.

137 BPM is close to twice 68.5 BPM.

A listener may map the Pulse metrically rather than literally.

KLAAT therefore needs a METRIC-PROXIMITY model that tests simple integer relationships such as 1:1, 2:1, and 1:2 before declaring a listener “far” from Ankora.

6. AMASTACIA CHANGES THE TEMPORAL CONTEXT

SMT research often uses taps, tones, metronomes, or conventional music.

KLAAT does not present Ankora in isolation.

Amastacia creates a sustained Field that can alter attention, event grouping, perceived density, and the salience of Ankora’s attacks.

The same physical Ankora tempo may therefore be experienced differently under:

ANKORA ALONE AMASTACIA + ANKORA DENSE AMASTACIA SPARSE / OPEN AMASTACIA

That makes Amastacia part of the temporal experiment even though she is not the Pulse.

7. GROUND ANCHOR — MEASURE BEFORE CAPTURE

Ground Anchor is the ideal place to establish a listener baseline.

Before Ankora enters, measure a brief spontaneous tapping or comfortable stepping reference outside the musical task.

Do not tell the listener that KLAAT is trying to match them.

Then let Amastacia establish the Field.

Ground Anchor now has two timing layers:

LISTENER BASELINE and KLAAT FIELD WITHOUT EXTERNAL PULSE.

This gives Threshold Gate a genuine before/after comparison.

8. THRESHOLD GATE — THE EXTERNAL CLOCK ARRIVES

Ankora’s entrance is not merely the addition of percussion.

It introduces an external periodic constraint into a system that already has endogenous timing tendencies.

Threshold Gate can therefore be reframed as:

FIELD → EXTERNAL CLOCK.

The key measurement is not whether the listener instantly “entrains.”

It is how movement, tapping, sway, attention, and perceived organization change after the Pulse becomes legible.

Some listeners may lock quickly. Some may adopt half-time. Some may remain relatively independent. Some may show no overt movement at all.

No single response is proof of deeper KLAAT engagement.

9. RISING PULSE — DENSITY WITHOUT TEMPO CHANGE

This is where KLAAT’s current performance language becomes especially useful.

Rising Pulse does not require continual acceleration.

Ankora can keep the same underlying clock while subdivisions, accents, omissions, and density change around it.

That lets KLAAT test whether the listener remains coupled to the underlying Pulse even as surface complexity increases.

A listener may stop visibly tapping every event but preserve a slower metrical relation.

Therefore:

LOSS OF OVERT 1:1 MOVEMENT ≠ LOSS OF TEMPORAL ORGANIZATION.

The correct question is whether a stable relationship survives at some metrical level.

10. RETURN PATH — DOES THE BODY KEEP THE CLOCK?

When Ankora disappears, KLAAT gets a rare experimental opportunity.

If the listener has been moving, ask what happens immediately after the external Pulse is removed.

Does spontaneous movement continue near Ankora’s period? Return toward pre-KLAAT SMT? Continue at half-time? Stop immediately? Drift gradually?

This is a synchronization-continuation problem embedded naturally in the four-phase architecture.

Return Path can therefore reveal temporal aftereffects without adding anything to the music.

11. ACTIVATION IS NOT SYNCHRONIZATION

KLAAT’s aim is activation, not sedation.

But synchronization itself is not equivalent to activation.

A listener can synchronize accurately while bored. A listener can be highly activated without moving. A listener can prefer a tempo without synchronizing strongly. A listener can move vigorously at a metrical level different from the nominal beat.

So KLAAT must keep separate:

SYNCHRONIZATION ACCURACY MOVEMENT MAGNITUDE TEMPO PREFERENCE BODILY IMPACT ATTENTIONAL ENGAGEMENT ACTIVATION

If those measures correlate in KLAAT, that becomes evidence.

They cannot be defined as one another.

12. INDIVIDUAL DIFFERENCE MAY BE SIGNAL, NOT NOISE

The strongest implication of SMT research is methodological.

Between-listener variability may not merely be messy data.

It may explain why identical KLAAT passages produce very different responses.

A participant near Ankora’s metric neighborhood might show one response trajectory while someone far from it shows another.

If so, averaging them together could erase the mechanism.

KLAAT should therefore preserve listener-level trajectories before calculating group averages.

13. ESTABLISHED EVIDENCE

Humans exhibit spontaneous motor tempo, measurable through self-paced rhythmic movement.

SMT varies substantially between individuals and is not one universal adult value.

SMT also varies within individuals according to task and contextual factors; repeated measurement is preferable to one trial.

Spontaneous motor tempo contributes to preferred music tempo in controlled research.

Preferred/produced tempo proximity can modulate motor-system responses, but the direction and magnitude of that modulation can differ among individuals.

Intrinsic rhythmicity/SMT predicts aspects of externally paced sensorimotor synchronization.

Humans can synchronize across a broad musical-tempo range, and musical beat perception permits hierarchical/metrically related interpretations.

14. CONTEXT-DEPENDENT EVIDENCE

Finger-tapping SMT is not automatically identical to whole-body sway, stepping, frame-drum response, or movement during immersive performance.

Tempo preference does not equal activation.

Motor-cortex excitability is not a direct measure of subjective bodily impact or artistic engagement.

A tempo close to SMT is not universally superior.

The optimal relationship may depend on whether KLAAT wants ease, tension, synchronization, surprise, or sustained attention.

The ~137 BPM estimate remains a calibration anchor until formally measured from a defined KLAAT performance.

15. PLAUSIBLE / TESTABLE KLAAT HYPOTHESES

Listener SMT and metric proximity to Ankora will explain some between-listener variation in spontaneous movement and Pulse-locking.

Listeners may couple to ~137 BPM at 1:1, half-time, or other simple metrical relationships rather than literal beat-for-beat movement.

Threshold Gate will produce stronger temporal reorganization in some listeners when Ankora enters near a metrically compatible relation to their baseline.

Rising Pulse can increase perceived activation through density and prediction changes while the underlying synchronization period remains stable.

Return Path may reveal a measurable aftereffect: movement may temporarily preserve Ankora’s clock before drifting toward baseline.

Amastacia’s Field may alter the salience and stability of Ankora coupling without changing Ankora’s physical tempo.

16. SPECULATION

KLAAT’s characteristic fast Ankora Pulse may work partly because many listeners can map it onto a slower half-time bodily period.

The contrast between a fast audible Pulse and slower whole-body movement may become a core KLAAT feature.

Threshold Gate may feel unusually strong when Ankora captures a listener from an endogenous timing state into a new but metrically compatible clock.

Listeners far from Ankora’s metric neighborhood may experience useful productive tension rather than weaker KLAAT.

These are hypotheses and artistic possibilities, not findings.

17. UNSUPPORTED CLAIMS

137 BPM is the natural human tempo.

137 BPM entrains everyone.

A listener whose SMT is close to 137 BPM will have a deeper KLAAT experience.

Visible sway proves neural entrainment.

Synchronization proves activation, trance, autonomic regulation, healing, or therapeutic benefit.

SMT is a person’s fixed biological frequency.

Finger tapping reveals the one tempo the whole body wants.

Matching Ankora to SMT is inherently better than deliberate mismatch.

New rules:

137 BPM IS A KLAAT REFERENCE, NOT A HUMAN CONSTANT.

MEASURE THE LISTENER’S CLOCK BEFORE CLAIMING CAPTURE BY KLAAT’S CLOCK.

SYNCHRONIZATION IS AN OUTCOME, NOT A SYNONYM FOR ACTIVATION.

PRESERVE INDIVIDUAL RESPONSE SHAPES BEFORE AVERAGING THEM AWAY.

19. CORE-KLAAT TRANSLATION

This mechanism requires no electronic composition system.

The research tools can measure tapping and movement, but the musical variable remains entirely core KLAAT:

AMASTACIA — the Drone / Field. ANKORA — the Pulse / external clock. BREATH — phrase and continuity. SILENCE — temporal absence. REPETITION — prediction scaffold. DURATION — time for coupling and drift. ROOM — acoustic context. BODY — listener movement and timing.

If the mechanism survives testing, the compositional lesson is not “use a metronome or AI-generated rhythm.”

It is learning how long Ankora must establish a clock, when to preserve it, when to densify around it, when to violate it, and what happens when it disappears.

20. KRX-063 — THE INTERNAL-CLOCK TEST

Recruit repeated listener sessions if possible.

Before KLAAT, obtain three short spontaneous motor tempo trials using the same simple instruction: tap at a comfortable, natural, regular rate. If whole-body movement is central to the question, add a separate comfortable stepping or sway-rate condition rather than assuming finger SMT equals body SMT.

Measure actual Ankora BPM from the performed excerpt.

Create four short KLAAT excerpts using Amastacia and Ankora only:

A — Ankora tempo near the listener’s measured SMT or nearest practical metrical equivalent. B — approximately 10–15% slower than that relation. C — approximately 10–15% faster. D — the canonical KLAAT ~137 BPM condition.

Keep the four-phase logic identical:

GROUND ANCHOR — Amastacia alone. THRESHOLD GATE — Ankora establishes a simple clock. RISING PULSE — density/subdivision increases while underlying BPM remains fixed. RETURN PATH — Ankora disappears; Amastacia remains.

Record tapping or unobtrusive movement when practical, but do not require continuous movement if the goal is natural response.

Primary measures:

time to stable synchronization; phase variability; selected metric level; movement magnitude; Pulse clarity; bodily impact; activation; preference; post-Ankora continuation tempo and drift.

The decisive analysis is not “which BPM wins?”

It is:

DOES DISTANCE FROM THE LISTENER’S CURRENT MOTOR-TEMPO NEIGHBORHOOD PREDICT HOW ANKORA IS ACQUIRED, MAINTAINED, AND RELEASED — AND DOES THAT RELATION EXPLAIN BODILY IMPACT OR ACTIVATION BEYOND BPM ALONE?

If yes, Listener Temporal Baseline becomes a real KLAAT moderator.

If synchronization changes but bodily impact does not, keep the timing result and do not inflate it into an activation mechanism.

If SMT adds no predictive value under KLAAT conditions, drop it from the core model.

That negative result would be useful too.

RECOMMENDED NEXT DIRECTION

Session 63 opens a direct route back from measurement science into composition. Once the listener-tempo question is tested, the strongest synthesis target is no longer a generic “tactile orchestration” system. It is a CORE KLAAT TEMPORAL GRAMMAR: how Amastacia establishes time without beat, how Ankora imposes a clock, how repetition makes that clock predictive, how Rising Pulse changes surface density without losing the underlying scaffold, and how Return Path tests the persistence of that clock after Ankora is gone.

That grammar remains didgeridoo and drum.

The scientific layer measures the relationship.

The music remains KLAAT.
