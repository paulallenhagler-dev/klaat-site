SESSION 30 — ATTACK TRANSIENTS
ARCHIVAL RECONSTRUCTION — NOT VERBATIM


Core lesson
The first milliseconds of a sound carry strong information about identity, timing, and impact. Attack and decay shape how precisely an event is localized in time and how urgent it feels.


At the same BPM and approximate loudness, rounded Ankora attacks can feel softer and less time-locked than sharp attacks.


Evidence boundary
Established: transient structure strongly influences timbre and temporal perception. Safety boundary: percussion can create high peak levels even when average loudness feels manageable.


KLAAT test
Hold Ankora near 135–140 BPM and alternate rounded, normal, and sharp attacks. Collect ratings for urgency, perceived speed, physical impact, precision, activation, and loudness.
